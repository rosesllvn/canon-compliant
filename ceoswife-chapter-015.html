Chapter 15

Falling, and Caught 掉落与接住

July 2026

~4 min read

That evening, after dinner, Jiang Qi and Ke Yan sat in the rattan chairs out on the balcony, taking in the night view, Jiang Qi leaning back against Ke Yan's chest in the summer breeze.

"Do you still remember our university days?" Jiang Qi asked suddenly. "We used to sit like this in the wind, too."

"I remember. You were a good deal more of a scatterbrain back then — always tripping over your own feet."

"Heh. My mom says she had me right after taking a fall, too." Jiang Qi couldn't help laughing aloud.

Ke Yan reached over and pinched his nose. "Still laughing? Lucky you've got a strong life-force and came through all right." Otherwise I'd never have met you. That part he didn't say aloud, only to himself.

Jiang Qi gripped his hand tight. "Mm — lucky indeed." Otherwise I'd never have met you. That part Jiang Qi kept to himself as well.

Hands clasped fast, neither noticed how very close their two hearts ran.

✦   ✦   ✦

Morning, at the Jiang home.

Jiang Qi and his parents sat at the table over congee and youtiao, while out in the living room Jiang Lü packed her schoolbag, hollering, "I'm going to be late, I'm going to be late, ahh~~ why didn't anyone wake me earlier!"

"I already called you — you wouldn't get up. And another thing, Jiang Lü: can't you pack your bag the night before so you don't leave things behind? I'm not running them over to your school again. Last term I delivered forgotten books a dozen-odd times; this term you sort it out yourself." Their mother scolded; the girl really was a handful.

"What a racket so early. So sleepy." Jiang Bu came out yawning, plopped down lolling his head, and started on breakfast.

"Jiang Bu, look at the state of you. Yun Hui will be put off if she sees you like that."

"My wife likes me just this way."

"Calling her your wife when she's not even married in yet?" Jiang Lü jeered on her way out, and was gone.

"Hmph — that girl, no respect for her elders," Jiang Bu said through his teeth.

"I'm full." Jiang Qi, who'd listened to it all, carried his bowl to the kitchen, then came back and checked the time. "I should be getting going too."

"Wait a bit — let your brother drive you."

"No need, I'll go on my own." And he went to his room and hurried back out hauling his suitcase.

"That boy — why's he off in such a rush?"

"The children are all grown now." Jiang Qi's dad, silent all this while, sighed the words.

Jiang Qi's dormitory was on the fifth floor. By the time he'd hauled the heavy suitcase up all five flights he was nearly ready to collapse. He found the room, opened the door, and discovered someone already there. "Hello, I'm Jiang Qi," he offered politely.

"Hello, I'm Liu Hao — I hope we'll get on well." Liu Hao gave him a playful wink. Baby-faced, his skin fair and soft, more tender than a girl's, with a warm, eager manner: that was Jiang Qi's first impression of him.

Jiang Qi chose a lower bunk and made up the bed — lower, for fear of rolling off in his sleep — and Liu Hao took a lower bunk too. They got their luggage squared away.

"Jiang Qi, I'm going to step out for a bit. Let's have lunch together at noon."

"Mm, all right."

Once Liu Hao had gone, Jiang Qi grew bored. He wandered out and leaned on the railing, taking in the campus, idly toying with the necklace his mother had prayed over for him — she'd gotten it at a mountain temple when he fell gravely ill as a child, and he'd worn it ever since. Who'd have thought that today the clasp would loosen and it would slip free. "Ah — my necklace!" He reached to catch it, and missed.

Down below, Ke Yan heard the cry, looked up just in time to see something tumbling out of the air, put out a hand — and the necklace dropped into his palm.

Seeing a boy on the fourth floor catch it, Jiang Qi called out, "That's mine — don't throw it away!" And he turned and ran for the fourth floor.
