Chapter 163

Scolded Silly by Mother Yang 被杨妈骂傻了

July 2026

~6 min read

They reached the hotel before mealtime. Yang Shaoyu woke Yang Shaowen, who came around groggily and wiped the drool from his mouth.

Father and Mother Yang were on their way; the eldest brother would be a while leaving the office.

The three went up to wait, and since not everyone had arrived, ordered tea and small dishes to nibble while they waited.

Yang Shaowen, perhaps not having eaten well on the plane, fell on the dishes the moment they came.

Lin Zirui filled the three's teacups and ate a couple of peanuts.

The hotel had no private rooms. Instead, lifelike bamboo and vines divided the main hall into elegant, secluded partitions. Diners could glimpse the room through the greenery, but outsiders would have to look deliberately to see in.

Father and Mother Yang and the eldest brother's family arrived shortly; they ordered, and as the food came Mother Yang looked Yang Shaowen over. "When are you going to change out of that getup — it's so unsightly."

"Mom, what do you know? This look turns heads. Girls on the street stared at me." Yang Shaowen thought he looked exceptionally sharp.

Mother Yang couldn't help rolling her eyes; seeing the gentle Mother Yang suddenly make such a face, Lin Zirui blinked, and Yang Shaoyu teased her, "Mom, you've startled Zirui."

"Really?" Her face went back to normal.

"No, Mom." Lin Zirui, lest she feel embarrassed.

"Good." She smiled. "Oh — come home with Shaoyu for the New Year." She knew well how Lin Zirui's family treated him, and pitied the boy.

"All right, I'll come home with Shaoyu for the New Year." Lin Zirui smiled, though memories of past holidays surfaced: his early years at home, then recent New Year's Eves spent on night shift while only his brothers called. His parents had seemed not to care whether he was there.

"Zirui — if you're not going home for the New Year, will you go pay a New Year's visit to your parents with Shaoyu?" Though Father Yang knew Lin Zirui's parents treated him poorly, the New Year still called for at least one visit.

"No need — they… probably don't want to see me, and I don't want to go back." Lin Zirui dropped his head and sipped his tea.

"Then don't. Go when you want to see them." Yang Shaoyu put an arm around his shoulders.

"Right — if going home for the New Year only means the same treatment, how sad that would be. Better to spend it at your own home." Mother Yang agreed.

The food came then, and the eldest brother called everyone to eat. "Dig in, we're hungry."

Everyone took up chopsticks, Yang Shaowen eating especially fast.

In the partition next door, Father and Mother Lin had ordered several dishes and touched none, ears pricked toward Zirui and the Yang family's conversation.

Hearing the suggestion that Zirui pay a New Year's visit with Yang Shaoyu, Father and Mother Lin were displeased — they hadn't even agreed to Zirui being with a man, so why should Yang Shaoyu pay them a New Year's call?

But Zirui's next words turned their faces strange — what did he mean, didn't want to go back?

Sourness rose in Mother Lin. What did that woman mean, saying that seeing them would only make Zirui sad? Sheer nonsense.

Father Lin gripped her clenched hand. "Don't get angry yet."

"Don't get angry? I think she went too far — that's as good as telling Zirui not to come home." Saying it, Mother Lin forgot that when she'd learned Lin Zirui loved a man, she herself had said the Lin family had no such shameful child.

Worked up, Mother Lin's voice rose a little, and next door Lin Zirui's chopsticks paused mid-reach — had he just heard his mother's voice?

"What is it? Not to your taste?" Yang Shaoyu saw Lin Zirui stop eating.

"No — eat." Lin Zirui thought he'd misheard.

Once they'd mostly finished, Lin Zirui got up to use the restroom.

Coming back, the next partition's door opened and its occupants stepped out. Lin Zirui paid them no mind and moved to go around them.

But he was blocked; looking, he found it was Father and Mother Lin. "Dad, Mom?"

He was stunned to run into his parents.

"Zirui, come in." The partitions weren't soundproof, and Father and Mother Yang mistook his call for them.

Mother Lin's face soured further; she glanced at him. "Come home with me — I have something to say to you."

"Sorry, I can't — someone's waiting for me." Lin Zirui's manner was a little distant.

"Are their words more important than mine and your father's? I forbid you to keep seeing this Yang Shaoyu."

"That I can't do. I'm going in now — suit yourselves." His manner unchanged.

"Zirui?" Yang Shaoyu came out to find him, paused at the sight of Father and Mother Lin, recovered quickly. "Come in and sit." And he drew Lin Zirui in.

Father and Mother Lin exchanged a look, and in the end followed.

"Sit." Seeing the Lin parents, Mother Yang shed the casualness she had with family, turning both elegant and commanding.

They sat; Mother Lin glanced at Mother Yang and got to the point. "I've come to take Zirui home."

"Forgive my bluntness, but Zirui doesn't want to go home with you. Don't be so quick to blame him. You were the ones who drove him out, yet now you expect him to return without considering how he feels?"

Mother Lin was struck speechless by those few words.

"The reason we drove Zirui out is your son — if he hadn't insisted on being with him, we'd never have driven Zirui out." Father Lin spoke, rebutting.

Father Yang frowned. "That's more wrong still. We're Shaoyu's parents — though we didn't agree at first, we cherished our child and agreed to them being together. How is it you two don't know to cherish your own child, only press him into a corner?"

"Our family's affairs need no meddling from you." Father Lin looked at Lin Zirui, signaling him to leave with them.

Yang Shaoyu tightened his arm around Lin Zirui's shoulders.

Lin Zirui glanced at Father Lin. "I won't go home with you like this."

Father Lin's face darkened.

"I told you not to pressure the child. Why not examine yourselves? Don't think I know nothing of your family. You ignored Zirui throughout his childhood and cared only for Lin Le, and now you would force him into something he doesn't want. What kind of parents are you? Selfish, heartless, vain, and self-righteous—I can hardly bear to watch." Mother Yang was truly angry now, all elegance gone in her withering attack.

Father and Mother Lin sat dumbly under it, unable to get a word out — scolded silly by Mother Yang.
