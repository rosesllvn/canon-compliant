The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

One day, two, three, four, five, six, seven — from that night on, Jiang Qi went a full week without seeing Ke Yan anywhere at school. No library, no dorm, no sign of him on campus at all. Everyone was discussing where he'd gone, the girls most fiercely of all.

Jiang Qi lay slumped over the desk, listening to the speculation, and sighed. Where on earth has he gone? Not a word, either — we're friends, at least, aren't we? They'd read together, eaten together; he knew Ke Yan's name, knew he liked to read, knew he was good-looking, knew he had a house off campus, knew his phone number—

He sighed again. He didn't know Ke Yan's phone number. Wait — he sat up straight. He knew Ke Yan's name; but did Ke Yan know his? He thought hard. He didn't seem to have told him — no, he seemed to have — he couldn't remember. He flopped back down and sighed. Argh.

Liu Hao, behind him, saw the sigh after sigh and reached over to pat his shoulder. Jiang Qi turned. "Jiang Qi, are you all right?"

"I'm fine~~" There was no strength in it at all.

"Yeah?" Not in the least convincing. Liu Hao picked up the book on the desk. "Let's go eat, then."

"All right." Jiang Qi took his books and went with him.

In the canteen, Liu Hao watched the listless Jiang Qi across from him. "Jiang Qi — did you get your heart broken?"

"No." Jiang Qi found it odd he'd think so, and touched his own face. Do I really look like someone fresh off a breakup?

"What breakup? Who got dumped?" Meng Qing sat down with his food.

"Nobody — I just think Jiang Qi looks heartbroken."

"Jiang Qi, did you really get dumped?"

"Honestly, no. I've never even dated — how would I get dumped?"

Meng Qing waved his chopsticks, mouth stuffed, words muffled. "Getting your heart broken doesn't necessarily mean you dated and split up."

Jiang Qi set down his chopsticks. "I'm full — you two take your time." And he walked off.

Meng Qing watched him go and shook his head. "Something's off."

"I think so too," Liu Hao agreed.

Toward evening, Jiang Qi stood in front of the library, looking up at its sign. Should I go in? He didn't know whether Ke Yan would come today. What if he doesn't come again? He didn't like the taste of waiting for someone — especially not knowing whether the person would come at all.

He hesitated, then turned and walked away — took two steps — stopped. Walked back, went in. Maybe he'll come today. If I leave now I might just miss him.

Outside, hidden behind a tree, Meng Qing and Liu Hao watched him go in. Meng Qing loathed libraries above all — a great crowd sitting in silence, staring at books for half a day; for someone who loved noise and chatter, unbearable.

Seeing him still standing there in a daze, Liu Hao tugged him. "Come on."

The two went in and found not a soul. "This is great," Meng Qing marveled. They searched a good while without finding Jiang Qi, until Liu Hao leaned against a shelf. "Where'd he run off to?"

"Here, found him." Meng Qing pointed.

Jiang Qi was flipping listlessly through a book, taking in nothing. Why is it like this? He didn't understand. He buried his head in the book — then lifted it and got a terrible fright, jerking back against his chair: Meng Qing and Liu Hao had sat down soundlessly across from him. He recovered. "What are you two doing?"

"That's our question for you. Out with it — tell us and we'll help you analyze it."

Jiang Qi looked at the two unreliable faces, thought it over, and decided saying it aloud might help. "Mm — I agreed with someone to read together, and then he up and vanished, not a word to me."

"Are you sure you agreed with him," Meng Qing asked sharply, "and it's not just what you assumed on your own?"

The question stumped Jiang Qi. He thought carefully — there hadn't actually been any agreement; he'd simply waited in the library. "There wasn't an agreement, I suppose..."

Meng Qing spread his hands. "Well, there you are. No arrangement, so naturally the person wouldn't tell you before disappearing."

"That's awfully hasty," Liu Hao groaned, "closing the case that fast."

Meng Qing ignored him; a thought flashed through his mind, and he said excitedly, "Jiang Qi — you weren't reading with a girl, were you? Are you this crushed because you've fallen for her?"

The words sent Jiang Qi's heart racing despite him. Like Ke Yan? No, no, no, how could that be — he's a man, it's certainly not that. But then why is my heart pounding so hard? He pressed a hand to his chest. It's just Meng Qing's words giving me a fright. It has to be. He denied the matter to himself, said nothing, slowly stood, and walked out — leaving his book on the desk, and the two gaping after him.

On the little path lined with nameless trees, Jiang Qi went up and down again and again, an unsorted tangle of feeling in his chest — strange, and unsettling. But having no experience of love, he soon chose to stop thinking about it, and filed the whole thing away as nothing more than having been startled.

That night the weary Jiang Qi came back to the dorm building, and passing the fourth floor glanced over out of habit — and his eyes went wide with delight. Ke Yan, leaning against the railing outside the dorms. Jiang Qi walked over, step by step. "Where did you go?"

Ke Yan glanced at the overjoyed face. "Had something to deal with."

A beat, and he turned to go back in. Jiang Qi caught at his hem. "My name's Jiang Qi. Did you know?"

Ke Yan didn't stop, only answered evenly, "I know. You told me."

And Jiang Qi's vexation melted away, the day's troubles with it, dissolved into those few words.
