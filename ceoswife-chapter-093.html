Chapter 93

Likes Children 喜欢小孩

July 2026

~8 min read

Lin Zirui gave a yawn, meaning to head home from work; back in the office, the moment he opened the door he saw Yang Shaoyu sitting inside. Lin Zirui wiped a hand over his face — how had he come this early today; he'd thought that if he got off early he wouldn't have to see Yang Shaoyu.

He took off his white coat, ignoring the radiant smile Yang Shaoyu beamed at him.

Coat on, keys in hand, Lin Zirui made to leave; Yang Shaoyu stopped him. "Don't go home yet; come somewhere with me."

"Don't you have work today?" Lin Zirui frowned.

"It's Saturday; I'm off."

"Oh, I still have to finish my shift; I'll go eat first." Lin Zirui made an excuse.

"I already asked; you're off now, no night shift today."

Lin Zirui glared at Yang Shaoyu.

Yang Shaoyu took Lin Zirui's hand and walked him out. "Come on."

Lin Zirui shook the hand free; Yang Shaoyu didn't insist, and walked side by side with him.

"How is it — changed yet?" Ke Yan knocked on the fitting-room door.

"Mm, done." Jiang Qi's voice came from inside.

Jiang Qi opened the door and came out; Ke Yan saw Jiang Qi in the white suit. Jiang Qi straightened his clothes. "Do I really have to wear this?"

"Mm." Ke Yan fixed Jiang Qi's tie for him.

He pinched Jiang Qi's cheek. "Don't be nervous when you see Mom and Dad today."

Jiang Qi's face stiffened; watching, Ke Yan found it funny. "Let's go."

"Mm." Jiang Qi picked up the gift and followed.

When the two reached the Ke house the venue was nearly all set up; Ke Yan led Jiang Qi inside. It was Jiang Qi's first time at Ke Yan's home; he looked around — it really was big.

"I'll show you around in a bit." Ke Yan held Jiang Qi's hand as he gazed about.

"Mm."

"Where are the flowers? Are they almost here? Bring the streamers." A voice came from a room, the footsteps suggesting someone was about to come out.

Jiang Qi let go of the hand Ke Yan held.

"Young master." Out came an elderly housekeeper, followed by three or four women in servants' clothes, all carrying things, presumably for decorating the party.

The old man in the housekeeper's attire called Ke Yan "young master" respectfully.

"Uncle Fu." Ke Yan addressed the old man as Uncle Fu.

"I was just wondering when you'd get home, young master." Uncle Fu spoke to Ke Yan with a smile; listening from the side, Jiang Qi could tell the two were on close terms.

"And this is..." Uncle Fu looked at Jiang Qi.

"He's my—"

"I'm his friend." Jiang Qi cut Ke Yan off, afraid he'd say something like this is the one I love.

In fact Jiang Qi really did know Ke Yan well — Ke Yan had been about to introduce Jiang Qi to Uncle Fu as his sweetheart.

"Hello." Uncle Fu greeted Jiang Qi with a smile. "Young master, you two go on in; I'll get back to work." With that Uncle Fu gave Ke Yan a slight bow and led the others off.

Jiang Qi and Ke Yan then saw Lan Ruyin on the sofa holding Xiao Ke, playing with him.

Ke Shao was off to one side eating fruit.

Ke Shao saw Ke Yan and Jiang Qi come in first. "Xiao Yan, you're back?"

Lan Ruyin looked up with a smile, then saw the Jiang Qi beside Ke Yan; her smile stiffened, and she put it away.

Seeing Lan Ruyin like this, Jiang Qi got more nervous; he bowed to her. "Auntie, hello — it's been a long time."

At Jiang Qi's "it's been a long time," Lan Ruyin remembered the meal Jiang Qi had cooked before; it was only after that meal that she'd realized how good Auntie Li's cooking really was.

"Sit." Perhaps because she'd recalled that meal, Lan Ruyin's manner toward Jiang Qi softened a little.

Jiang Qi sat; Ke Yan sat beside Jiang Qi, and Lan Ruyin shot Ke Yan a look — must they be this clingy?

"Here? Have some fruit." Ke Shao, reclining on the sofa, beckoned Jiang Qi to have fruit.

Jiang Qi wanted to say thank you, but suddenly didn't know what to call Ke Shao.

He looked at Ke Yan, thinking it over.

"Call her sis." Ke Yan had been watching Jiang Qi the whole time; seeing Jiang Qi turn to him for help, his mood lifted at once — this scene was exactly like bringing your wife home to meet the family, the wife turning to the husband for help.

"Sis." Jiang Qi called Ke Shao "sis."

"Mm." Ke Shao answered, drawing a glare from Lan Ruyin.

Paying her mother no mind at all, Ke Shao took a fresh silver fork and speared a piece of honeydew for Jiang Qi.

Lan Ruyin gave a snort and shoved the sleeping Xiao Ke she'd been holding into Ke Shao's arms.

Ke Shao wasn't annoyed, and said with a smile, "Mom, must you be like this."

"Here, Jiang Qi, you hold him." With that Ke Shao pushed Xiao Ke into Jiang Qi's arms.

Jiang Qi caught Xiao Ke, stiff all over, holding the soft little baby in his arms, terrified that a heavy hand might hurt him.

"Ah, ah, mm." Probably uncomfortable in Jiang Qi's hold, Xiao Ke woke before long, squirmed in Jiang Qi's arms, made fussing noises, his little mouth puckering, looking about to cry.

"Ke Yan, Ke Yan, what do I do." Jiang Qi looked anxiously at Ke Yan, then remembered Ke Yan was as inexperienced as he was.

"Here, hold him like this." Ke Shao guided Jiang Qi's hands — one supporting Xiao Ke's bottom, the other bracing his back.

"He can crawl now, but just-woken from a nap he needs holding a while to wake up." Ke Shao played with the just-woken Xiao Ke while talking to Jiang Qi.

Jiang Qi held Xiao Ke and gently rocked him; the Xiao Ke in his arms felt so small and cute.

Probably held comfortably now by Jiang Qi, Xiao Ke wasn't shy and didn't cry, lying against Jiang Qi's shoulder going ah-ah-ah.

Jiang Qi carefully shifted the one lying on his back into another position, setting Xiao Ke on his lap, and held Xiao Ke's little hands, gesturing with them — sure enough, so small.

"Miss, your friend's here." A servant came to bring Jiang Qi tea and spoke to Ke Shao.

"All right." Ke Shao got up to go out, leaving Xiao Ke behind; Jiang Qi held Xiao Ke and watched Ke Shao's departing back, then looked down at Xiao Ke — well, he'd hold him a while longer then.

Lan Ruyin gave Jiang Qi a complicated look — he likes children this much? A pity he can't bear one, or she wouldn't object so. She glanced at her son, and Lan Ruyin was vexed — still watching Jiang Qi; from the moment they'd come in, except when speaking to her, his gaze hadn't once left him. Lan Ruyin still couldn't figure out what was so good-looking about Jiang Qi.

She set down her teacup and decided to go find her husband to console her "wounded" heart.

Seeing Lan Ruyin leave, Jiang Qi lowered his head and burrowed his face into Xiao Ke's neck for a sniff — so fragrant, a milky sweetness.

Tickled at the neck by Jiang Qi, Xiao Ke reached out a little hand and patted Jiang Qi's face.

"You like children this much?" Ke Yan asked after a silence.

"Mm." Jiang Qi nodded.

"..."

"Do you like children?" Seeing Ke Yan not answer, Jiang Qi asked.

"I don't dislike them."

"..." Jiang Qi looked down at Xiao Ke, and after a long while said, "I'm a man; I can't give you a child. Would you — would you want one of your own?" His voice dropped low; if Ke Yan hadn't been sitting right beside him, he might not have heard.

Ke Yan went unusually still, then kissed Jiang Qi's forehead and held him. "I wondered whether, if we hadn't gotten together, you'd be married with children by now. Seeing how much you love them, I was afraid you might regret choosing me. The thought of you having a child with some woman made me want to kill her."

Held by Ke Yan, Jiang Qi went blank; he'd hardly ever seen Ke Yan so anxious and insecure — wasn't he always so confident?

"Heh heh..." Jiang Qi laughed softly in Ke Yan's arms. "I won't; don't worry."

"Ah, ah." Xiao Ke, sitting on Jiang Qi's lap, squeezed between the two and feeling about to be flattened, gave a couple of ah-ahs at them.

Hearing Xiao Ke, Jiang Qi quickly sat up straight, lowered his head, and kissed Xiao Ke's cheek.

Ke Yan watched, narrowing his eyes — who says having a child is necessarily a good thing?
