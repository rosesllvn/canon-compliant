Silly Daughter-in-Law 傻儿媳妇

July 2026

~6 min read

Lan Ruyin had not cooked in far too long. She meant to make something light that Ke Xin liked, but oil spat toward her hand. She jerked back, and the spatula clattered to the floor. Jiang Qi: "…"

Lan Ruyin: "…" After a beat of silence, she calmly picked the spatula up, rinsed it, and went on frying.

The dish was finished, somehow, but besides tasting off it was a touch too salty — and, worst of all, greasy.

Jiang Qi looked at the two dishes and thought, privately, they were about on par with his own cooking.

"A failure." Lan Ruyin surveyed the two greasy plates and thought that if she served these to her husband, one look would kill his appetite.

"Should we make them again?" Jiang Qi figured Ke Xin, being ill, would have no stomach for anything this greasy.

"Of course we redo them. You don't know — he's the pickiest eater; serve him this and he won't give it a second glance, let alone eat it." Lan Ruyin set the finished dishes aside. "Crack two eggs — we'll make steamed egg custard."

"Steamed egg custard?" Jiang Qi had eaten it but never made it. He took two eggs from the fridge and cracked them into a bowl — botching it a bit, some shell crumbling in.

Looking at the bits of shell stuck to the yolk, Jiang Qi silently took two more eggs and started over.

Eggs beaten, Lan Ruyin added salt, then stirred while pouring in water.

Jiang Qi watched and learned from the side. Steamed egg custard was tasty; he could make it himself sometime.

Though — however closely he studied, whatever Jiang Qi made would come out just as inedible.

Once the egg mixture was steaming, Lan Ruyin blanched and plated the vegetables without a drop of oil or pinch of salt.

When the custard had steamed, she dished up a bowl of rice and added a plate of the utterly plain, oil-free vegetables, and had Jiang Qi take it up. When he brought it in, Ke Xin was chatting with Ke Yan, though he looked rather tired, weak from the illness, low on energy.

"The food's ready." Jiang Qi set the tray on the small table in the room.

"You made this?" Ke Yan asked.

"Auntie made it." Jiang Qi answered obediently — and on the sly, sneaked a pinch at Ke Yan's waist.

"Ahem. Dad, come eat." Pinched, Ke Yan winced, cleared his throat, and fetched Ke Xin's coat.

Ke Xin put on the coat, came to the little table, and sat. He tried a spoonful of the custard first, frowned, and poured a cup of water to drink — the custard was both overcooked and too salty.

He picked up a piece of the bright-green vegetable and ate it; this time he didn't frown, but set down his chopsticks, wiped his mouth. "Take it away. Inedible."

With that he shed the coat and got back into bed to rest.

Ke Yan and Jiang Qi stepped out, Jiang Qi carrying the tray down. Spotting them, Lan Ruyin looked over. "He didn't eat?"

"Dad said it was too inedible." Ke Yan set the tray down for Jiang Qi.

Lan Ruyin didn't believe it — her frying might be poor, but her steamed custard was perfectly good.

But one taste and she spat it straight out. "That really was a misfire."

Jiang Qi snorted a laugh — Auntie was just like him, a terrible cook.

Lan Ruyin huffed.

Jiang Qi promptly stopped laughing.

Lan Ruyin threw up her hands. She'd made steamed egg custard all these years and he'd eaten one spoonful before quitting. Such a fussy eater.

So Lan Ruyin decided to call up her close friends and go out shopping.

With her gone, Jiang Qi and Ke Yan went back to a room for an afternoon nap.

Jiang Qi slept an hour or so before waking, stomach growling — and remembered he hadn't eaten lunch. He thought he'd go down for a bite. He turned to look at Ke Yan, still asleep, dropped a kiss on him, then got up, dressed, and went down to rustle up some food.

Too hungry to want to cook for himself, he asked someone to make it instead.

The food came quickly: pork with green peppers and a plate of stir-fried greens. Jiang Qi checked on Ke Yan, found him still asleep, and returned downstairs to eat, leaving half warm in the pot.

He tasted the pork-and-pepper first and found it very good — so why didn't Ke Xin like it?

Then it struck him: Ke Yan was exactly the same way. Father and son, alike after all.

Ke Yan came down only after Jiang Qi had finished; waking to find the person in his arms gone, and no longer sleepy, he'd come down.

"You're up — I saved some food for you." Jiang Qi brought out the dishes from the pot and dished Ke Yan a bowl of rice.

Ke Yan sat, took a bite, then ate on without much appetite.

Watching, Jiang Qi felt this, too, was just like Ke Xin.

He suddenly remembered Ke Xin had eaten next to nothing at midday before going to sleep, and wondered if he'd woken, whether he was hungry.

"Ke Yan, I'll go up and see if Dad's awake."

"All right, go on." Watching Jiang Qi head up, Ke Yan smiled. This was exactly what made Jiang Qi so likable. He wasn't worried anymore that his father would dislike Jiang Qi — it was simply too hard to manage.

Jiang Qi opened the door, poked his head in to look: Ke Xin still asleep.

He crept to the bed and felt Ke Xin's forehead. It no longer seemed hot. Jiang Qi checked his own, then Ke Xin's again, and finally decided the fever had broken.

"What are you doing?"

Yah! Jiang Qi jumped half out of his skin. How had he woken so suddenly?

Ke Xin had sensed someone enter and awakened to Jiang Qi comparing their foreheads, then sighing with relief.

He looked so foolish—the same age as Ke Yan, yet without his maturity. Ke Yan had protected him so well that Jiang Qi seemed almost untouched by the world.

"Un— Uncle, you're, you're awake."

See — stammering, even. Was he really so frightening?

"Pour me a cup of water."

"Oh — right." Jiang Qi poured a cup of warm water.

Ke Xin drank a couple of sips. "I'm hungry. Go see what there is to eat."

"All right, I'll go right now." Jiang Qi hurried down to find food.

Ke Xin gave a satisfied nod and picked up the books Jiang Qi had brought him to read.

Truth be told — having a silly daughter-in-law might not be so bad after all.
