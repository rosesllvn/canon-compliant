Chapter 13

Jiang Qi Treats Ke Yan to "a Good Meal" 江奇请柯言'吃菜'

July 2026

~3 min read

At the supermarket Jiang Qi bought nothing at all but a few heads of napa cabbage and a few potatoes.

Back home, Auntie Li took the bag and looked inside. Only cabbage and potatoes? Are we eating vegetarian today? "Jiang Qi, what dishes should I make?"

Jiang Qi answered her question with one of his own. "Is Ke Yan back?"

"Not yet — soon, I'd guess."

"Oh. Auntie Li — I'll do the cooking today."

"You'll cook~~?" Her voice climbed, quavering; she hadn't forgotten the disaster of the day Jiang Qi had helped her in the kitchen.

"Don't worry, these two dishes I can manage. I've made them plenty of times."

"Plenty of times?" she said, uncertain. "Surely not — for the Young Master to eat?"

"That's right."

"Ah?"

Seeing her still rooted there, Jiang Qi steered her toward the door. "Please, don't worry — I can manage. Head home early today."

"Then... I'll go, shall I?"

"Yes — goodbye."

Once she'd gone, Jiang Qi returned to the kitchen, rinsed the cabbage leaf by leaf and set the leaves to soak, then laid a potato on the board, lifted the cleaver, and hacked off its skin — a nice round potato turned small and square in an instant. He hacked through several more, then cut them into strips, and the strips finer.

Well — finer is generous; each was at least as thick as his little finger. On an ordinary day he'd have taken care to cut them properly thin. But today.

He turned on the flame, poured in a little oil, dumped in all the potatoes, splashed in the soy sauce he normally never touched, then tipped in half a bottle of salt in one go. Without waiting for it to cook through, he turned it out onto a plate.

He fished the cabbage from its soak, raised the knife — chop, chop — a few careless strokes, never minding whether he'd cut clean through; on some leaves the stem was severed while the leaf still hung on. Into the pot it went, and this time he was crueler still: he tipped in the whole jar of sugar, added water, brought it to a boil, and poured the "sweet cabbage" in beside the "salty potatoes" so the two dishes ran into one. Then he waited for Ke Yan to come home and taste it.

He carried the dish to the table — no rice, just the one plate.

Ke Yan walked in to find Jiang Qi standing at the table, lost in a daze. "What's wrong?"

Jiang Qi went over, took him by the hand and sat him down, pressed the chopsticks into his hand, and signaled him to eat.

Ke Yan looked at the horror on the table, paused, then took in Jiang Qi's unhappy face. "Did something happen today?"

"Eat first."

He looked at the dish, then at Jiang Qi, and with difficulty lifted a bite of cabbage — and spat it out the instant it touched his tongue. So sweet. He tried the potato. So salty.

Watching him, Jiang Qi couldn't help laughing out loud.

At the laugh Ke Yan relaxed. "What is it?"

"You really do attract the peach blossoms."

"Peach blossoms — who?"

"Wang Siqi. Today she even asked me to play matchmaker — to set the two of you up."

"Heh. She's really something." Ke Yan gave a mocking little laugh.

One look at that smile and Jiang Qi knew he was a touch angry. "What have you got to be angry about? I'm not even angry anymore."

"Because of all people, she should never have made you angry. If anything like last time happens again, I won't let her off."

Jiang Qi knew well enough what "last time" meant in Ke Yan's mouth. "Don't worry. It won't."
