One More Person to Love Him 多一个人爱他

July 2026

~6 min read

The next morning Jiang Qi got up listless, to find Ke Yan already risen. He sat on the bed a while, then went to brush his teeth, wash his face, and head down to make breakfast.

But downstairs he found breakfast already laid out on the table.

Toasted bread, milk, fried egg and ham.

He picked up the milk and drank — warm, the warmth reaching straight to his heart.

"Ke Yan?" he called. No answer.

Gone out this early? The milk was still warm; he couldn't have left long ago.

Jiang Qi went to the door and opened it to check whether Ke Yan was back, and saw him stepping out of the elevator with a bag in hand.

"Where'd you go?"

"To get something." Ke Yan came in, set the bag on the table, and went to the kitchen for a bowl.

"What is it?"

"Wontons." He tipped the boxed wontons into the bowl.

"Where'd you buy them? Nowhere near us sells them." Jiang Qi sniffed at it, pleased; being a high-end building, nowhere nearby sold this kind of thing.

"Had the driver buy them — I went down just now to get them." He handed Jiang Qi the spoon.

Jiang Qi fed Ke Yan a mouthful first, then started eating himself.

"Good?"

Jiang Qi nodded.

"Feel a little better, eating it?"

"Mm." He smiled, not so listless now.

"Eat more." Ke Yan began on his own breakfast.

After breakfast, the driver took them to the company's front entrance rather than the underground lot.

Sure enough, the moment Ke Yan stepped out, everyone looked over — and froze, staring at the white bandage around his head, the usual "Good morning, CEO" jamming in their throats, unsayable.

Ke Yan seemed neither to see nor feel it, and went into the elevator with Jiang Qi.

In the elevator — with the CEO aboard, no one dared get in, all of them waiting for the next one.

Inside, Jiang Qi couldn't help laughing.

Ke Yan reached out and pinched his cheek; seeing Jiang Qi laugh lifted his own mood a good deal.

The elevator soon arrived, and Jiang Qi got off to find Xiao An at the door.

"What is it?" Xiao An's expression was so peculiar that Jiang Qi couldn't help asking.

"I know everything, Jiang Qi — you and the CEO, keep at it, don't you dare break up, I'm rooting for you." Xiao An said it rather loudly; the staff in the office heard and turned to look at the two of them.

Jiang Qi blinked, then laughed. "I know, don't worry, we won't break up." He guessed Jiang Lü had told her.

To Jiang Qi's letting everyone overhear without the slightest evasion, Xiao An smiled and said, "Hang in there," and went off to work.

Jiang Qi went into his office, had his secretary Li Jing come report, and started the day's work.

At noon, Jiang Qi and Ke Yan ate together, after which Jiang Qi meant to rest a while in Ke Yan's office — but Ke Yan said he had to go out.

"Where to?"

"A lunch meeting." When Ke Yan lied, he wouldn't meet Jiang Qi's eyes; only when he spoke words of love did he hold his gaze.

"Oh — then whatever you do, no drinking." Jiang Qi glanced, worried, at the gauze on Ke Yan's forehead.

"Don't worry. I'm off, then."

"All right. Drive carefully."

Ke Yan went down to the lot, opened the car, and drove to the Jiang house.

It was far from the company, so the drive took a long while. He parked in the complex lot, went to the Jiang door, drew a breath, and pressed the bell.

After a moment someone came — this time it was Mother Jiang.

She opened the door and saw Ke Yan, paused, looked past him for Jiang Qi, and didn't find him.

"Auntie, Jiang Qi didn't come today." Ke Yan knew what she was looking for.

"Then what did you come for?" she said, stone-faced.

"I came alone today for the matter of Jiang Qi and me. I hope you'll allow us to be together."

"Allow? Whether I allow it or not, aren't you already together? Would my not allowing it make you break up?"

"We won't break up—we couldn't. I love Jiang Qi and want to spend my life caring for him. When he hurts, I hurt; when he cries, I want only to make him smile again. If you don't agree, he'll believe he did something wrong and grieve alone, but he still could never leave me. Seeing him like that breaks my heart." Ke Yan had never spoken this way to anyone but Jiang Qi. Yet she was Jiang Qi's mother, someone he cherished, so Ke Yan cared for her too.

"Fine words. Jiang Qi is my son, I know that — but you? You're the CEO of a company, you have power and money, no shortage of women wanting to marry you. Can you hold out against the temptations out there?" She didn't believe Ke Yan at all. "And two men together — what will the world think? People will point and whisper at you on the street. You may not care, but I care about my son."

"Auntie, we've been together seven years. We've endured the stares and whispers that follow even a little affection in public. Jiang Qi has never felt ashamed, only shy. I understand him. Your words yesterday hurt him deeply, yet he pretended to be cheerful for me. I know I've overprotected him, but I would rather continue. Yes, I'm a man—but what difference does that make? It only means one more person loves Jiang Qi. I beg you to agree." Ke Yan bowed deeply.

To plead with anyone like this — it was the first time in Ke Yan's life, sincere and wholehearted in his wish for her to agree.

For her part, Mother Jiang was a little moved by that line — one more person to love Jiang Qi — but she truly didn't want her son with a man, even as, in that instant, she felt there might be no one who loved Jiang Qi more than Ke Yan.

She suddenly grabbed the broom by the door and swung it at Ke Yan. "Get out. I'll be arranging matchmaking dates for Jiang Qi shortly — I will not agree to my son being with you."

"Jiang Qi won't go to them—" Ke Yan hadn't finished before several more hard whacks landed.

"Get out."

Helpless, Ke Yan let himself be driven down the stairs, rolled his somewhat sore shoulder, and made to go back to the office.

Suddenly his phone rang; one look at the caller ID and his heart went soft. He answered, and Jiang Qi's voice came through. "Ke Yan, is your lunch meeting over?"

"Mm, it's done. Just heading back to the office."

"You didn't drink, did you?"

"Don't worry, no drinking."

"Good. I'll let you go — drive carefully."

"All right, bye." Ke Yan hung up, looked up at the floor where the Jiangs lived. Not today — he'd just come again tomorrow.
