Chapter 168

The Wedding 结婚

July 2026

~8 min read

The next day Jiang Qi found the house silent, as if no one were home. Where had everyone gone?

At first he thought they'd simply not risen — but it was this late; how could Mother Jiang still be asleep?

He went to the Jiang parents' door and knocked; in the whole house there was no sound but his own knocking.

He opened their door — no one, as expected.

He called Father Jiang to ask where they'd gone, but couldn't get through; Mother Jiang's phone too. A little worried, he called Jiang Bu — maybe they were together.

Jiang Bu took a long while to answer. Jiang Qi asked, "Brother, where are you? Are Mom and Dad with you? Why can't I reach their phones?"

"Oh — yes, we're all together; don't worry, we're out, and won't be back today." Jiang Bu walked toward a quiet spot as he spoke, so Jiang Qi wouldn't hear anything.

"Where are you? You went out without a word?"

"Oh — something came up suddenly; you weren't up, so we didn't disturb you. Just stay home alone today." Jiang Bu said it guiltily and hung up.

"All right…" The line went dead before Jiang Qi finished. Ever since coming home, he'd had the odd feeling that everyone was hiding something from him. His instincts were dead on.

Alone at home, Jiang Qi ate the breakfast Mother Jiang had left him, then called Ke Yan — the second day of the New Year; what was Ke Yan up to?

But Ke Yan didn't pick up either.

Hm? Jiang Qi frowned, about to call again, when Ke Yan texted: I'm busy right now, see you tonight.

Reading it, Jiang Qi thought nothing much of it — just that Ke Yan's family had a lot on, and he'd probably come by tonight. Bored, he turned on the TV.

There was nothing worth watching—mostly reruns and dramas he didn't care for.

He yawned, and just then the doorbell rang. Setting down the remote, he went to open the door — but it wouldn't open.

He thought nothing of it and pressed the lock release, but still it wouldn't open. After a couple of dazed seconds he realized: the door was locked from outside.

Luckily, with a house key he could unlock it from inside.

He searched the woven basket on the shoe cabinet for the key — gone?

He remembered putting it there yesterday on coming home, but now it was missing.

The bell rang twice more.

Nothing for it; Jiang Qi told whoever it was, "Sorry — one moment."

Doubting his memory, he searched the house, but found no key, and could only explain through the door. It was Mother Jiang's friend, Auntie Zhang, bringing some specialty her son had bought, to drop off for Mother Jiang. Hearing Jiang Qi's explanation, Auntie Zhang said, half-laughing, "You've been locked in, have you? Never mind, I'll come another time." And left.

Auntie Zhang spoke loudly, so even through the door Jiang Qi heard her clearly, and knew she'd gone and would come again.

He sighed, helpless.

The key Jiang Qi couldn't find was, in fact, in Jiang Lü's bag — that morning, on her way out, she'd taken Jiang Qi's key and locked the door from outside.

Unable to leave, Jiang Qi spent the whole day in; after some TV he brewed a cup of milk tea and curled up in the study to read.

At noon he cooked a little rice and reheated two plates of last night's leftovers for lunch.

After, with nothing to do, he curled up in the study again.

Around four in the afternoon, Jiang Lü and Mother Jiang rushed home; Jiang Lü unlocked the door, and Mother Jiang dashed in, not even changing into slippers, to Jiang Qi's room — but didn't find him.

Coming out, she said to Jiang Lü, "Why isn't your brother in his room — surely he didn't go out?" She checked the time.

"He's definitely home — I took the key and locked the door from outside…" Jiang Lü said, heading for the study, and indeed found Jiang Qi the moment she opened it.

Seeing Jiang Lü, Jiang Qi asked, "You're back?"

Mother Jiang rushed into the study too, and without a word pulled the book from Jiang Qi's hands and tugged him up off the little sofa.

She drew him to his room, took a suit from the wardrobe, and handed it over. "Quick — go change."

"This is…" Jiang Qi looked at the white suit in his hands — the one he and Ke Yan had picked together, for…

His heart quickened. Could it be…

"All right, don't stand there — go change." Mother Jiang smiled and gave him a push.

In his room, Jiang Qi laid the suit on the bed and looked at it, his eyes growing hot. Slowly he undressed and put on the white shirt — and found, doing up the buttons, that his hands were trembling.

Dressed, he went down; outside the Jiang house waited a car Ke Yan had sent.

From the moment he got in, Jiang Qi watched out the window, calm on the surface, though only he knew how nervous he was inside. No wonder the family had been so odd these two days — and when had Ke Yan conspired with them…

The destination was far; it was dark by the time they arrived.

Jiang Qi got out, and Mother Jiang squeezed his hand. "Go on — Ke Yan's waiting for you."

Jiang Qi stood frozen at the scene before him, an indescribable wonder.

White and pale-green lights illuminated a forest. Flowers and shrubs crowded the path, glowing mushrooms nestled among them, and a little train waited at the center.

Beside the train stood a rabbit as tall as Jiang Qi.

Jiang Qi stared at the rabbit a dozen seconds, then stepped forward; seeing him come, the rabbit trotted over on two plump legs, took his hand, led him to the train, and gestured him aboard.

Jiang Qi climbed aboard. The open-topped train had a single passenger seat behind the driver, thickly cushioned in white and banked with pink flowers. A bouquet lay waiting for him.

Jiang Qi picked up the bouquet, sat, watched the rabbit climb into the front passenger seat, and the driver started the train.

It set off with a clack-clack, rolling slowly forward.

As the train moved, the lights dimmed, and Jiang Qi was startled to spot fireflies. Fireflies in winter?

The deeper the train went, the more fireflies there were, the densest gathered over a little lake spanned by a small bridge — and in that forest of white and pale-green light and fireflies, it looked romantic and otherworldly.

The train rolled on; light grew ahead, brighter the further it went, and Jiang Qi saw Ke Yan standing a little way off, smiling, waiting for him.

The train slowed to a stop; Ke Yan stepped up, took Jiang Qi's hand, and helped him down.

Ke Yan's hand was warm; Jiang Qi gripped it, and the two looked at each other and walked forward, step by step.

Before him stretched a red carpet, family and friends smiling from both sides and a priest waiting at the end. Wedding music filled the air. His palms grew damp and his heart pounded, equal parts nervous and moved.

Though he'd guessed the truth, seeing it made him understand the care Ke Yan had poured into every detail.

Walking that solemn, sacred red carpet with Ke Yan, Jiang Qi couldn't help thinking of their first meeting, their first words, their first held hands, their first kiss…

He looked at Ke Yan. "Do you remember the first time we met?"

"Of course. The first time I saw you, I thought you were a bit dazed — and odd."

"Odd how? I was trying to thank you back then, but you shut the door on me, not friendly at all."

Ke Yan found it funny, and squeezed his hand. "Whose fault was it, looking at me with that dazzled face? What man likes being looked at like that."

"True." Jiang Qi laughed.

"And back then I had no idea I'd come to love you — I'm glad I met you, loved you, and get to spend a life with you."

Jiang Qi was moved, the smile at his lips impossible to hold back, and said softly, "Me too."

They reached the priest, who smiled and spoke the vows:

"Ke Yan, will you love Jiang Qi from this day on, for poorer or richer, in sickness and in health, staying always by his side, honoring and cherishing him?"

"I will." Ke Yan looked at Jiang Qi, not a moment's hesitation.

"Jiang Qi, will you love Ke Yan from this day on, for poorer or richer, in sickness and in health, staying always by his side, honoring and cherishing him?"

"I will." Jiang Qi nodded, gripping Ke Yan's hand.

"You may kiss each other." Their rings had never left their hands, so there was no ring exchange.

Everyone applauded, blessing Jiang Qi and Ke Yan from the heart, more than a few cheering them on.

Ke Yan bent and kissed Jiang Qi, one arm at his waist, one hand cupping his face. Jiang Qi held Ke Yan's back, closed his eyes, and kissed him.

In this life, happiness was wherever Ke Yan stood beside him.

— The End —
