Chapter 69

Meeting Ke Yan's Mother? 与柯言妈妈见面了？

July 2026

~7 min read

Ke Yan had a much shorter journey home than Jiang Qi, though an errand for Lan Ruyin forced him to take a slight detour.

Even so, he got home a step ahead of Jiang Qi. He'd just come in and gotten his shoes off when Jiang Qi opened the door and came in.

When Jiang Qi arrived, Ke Yan was waiting in the entryway, his gaze tender. Jiang Qi loved that look.

He suddenly wanted to throw himself into Ke Yan's arms. No sooner had the thought occurred than he leaped, and Ke Yan caught him securely.

With his feet off the floor, Jiang Qi nestled comfortably in Ke Yan's strong arms.

They gazed at each other. Ke Yan tipped his head up, Jiang Qi bent his down, and they sank into a kiss. Ke Yan pressed him against the wall, one hand slipping beneath his shirt. Jiang Qi moaned as Ke Yan nipped his lips, answering with equal hunger.

Then... Jiang Qi caught sight of someone standing off to one side. At first he thought he was mistaken, but a second look left him stunned. Why was there a woman in their house?

Jiang Qi gaped at the woman; then he felt his belly go cool, and only then registered what he and Ke Yan were doing — Ke Yan kissing his neck, his shirt already rucked up halfway. Jiang Qi quickly pressed down the hand Ke Yan had on his chest and turned his neck aside to keep Ke Yan from kissing it.

"Ke, Ke Yan, someone's here." Jiang Qi gasped in alarm.

Ke Yan stopped, displeased, turned his head to look, and froze. "Mom? Why are you here?"

Mom? Jiang Qi's mind went blank, his whole self dumbstruck.

Lan Ruyin gave a cold snort. "If I hadn't come over I'd have missed this fine show."

At that Ke Yan helped Jiang Qi straighten his clothes; Jiang Qi's face was scarlet. Watching, Lan Ruyin felt worse than ever — she'd truly never seen her son this gentle.

She looked again at the Jiang Qi her son had been holding and kissing — looking foolish and dazed, blank and dumbstruck.

What was so good about this man — not especially handsome, not especially sharp-looking either; just thinking about it made her cross.

After overhearing Ke Yan's call, Lan Ruyin had guessed he was returning to Jiang Qi. She sent him on an errand and arrived first, intending to inspect the man herself. Finding Jiang Qi absent, she waited. Then came the sounds of doors opening and closing, a scuffle in the entryway, and sudden silence.

Curious, Lan Ruyin crept from the living room—and found Ke Yan pressing Jiang Qi against the wall, kissing and touching him.

She really couldn't contain her anger at the two of them. "Come in here."

Ke Yan took Jiang Qi's hand and walked him inside, only to find Jiang Qi stiff all over; he ruffled his hair. "Don't be nervous."

"Mm." Jiang Qi answered, but wore the face of a man going to his death.

Ke Yan couldn't help finding it funny, and gripped Jiang Qi's hand a little tighter.

In the living room, Lan Ruyin sat on the largest sofa; Ke Yan took Jiang Qi's hand and sat down, saying, "Mom, this is Jiang Qi. Jiang Qi, this is my mother."

Lan Ruyin said nothing; Jiang Qi called respectfully, "Hello, Auntie."

"Mm." Lan Ruyin answered coldly, then added, "Young people would do well to show some restraint."

The atmosphere was a bit awkward; Jiang Qi stood up and said, "I'll go pour water."

He fled, as it were, to the kitchen.

Seeing Jiang Qi gone, Lan Ruyin asked, "Are you really going to be with him."

"Yes." Ke Yan answered, his face earnest.

"What's so good about him?" Lan Ruyin truly couldn't see what about Jiang Qi Ke Yan could like.

"Everything about him is good."

"Hmph, I can't see it."

Just then Jiang Qi came out with honey-lemon water. Lan Ruyin took a sip. "You made this?"

"No, Auntie Li came and made it today."

"Mm. Right — I'm a bit tired; I'll go lie down a while. I'll eat dinner here." Lan Ruyin thought a moment and added, "You can cook, can't you?"

"Yes, I can."

Jiang Qi answered quickly.

Lan Ruyin said nothing more and went off to the bedroom to rest.

Jiang Qi cleared the table without a word; Ke Yan caught his hand, and Jiang Qi turned to give him a smile. "I'm fine." So saying, he carried the cups to the kitchen. Ke Yan watched him, then noticed he was walking with the same arm and leg swinging together — he hadn't realized Jiang Qi was this nervous.

Ke Yan went to the kitchen and washed the cups with Jiang Qi, then went with him to the supermarket to buy groceries.

At the supermarket, Jiang Qi kept asking Ke Yan, "What does Auntie like to eat? Does she like meat? Or seafood?"

Ke Yan watched him quietly a moment. "Can you cook it?" Jiang Qi's hand, picking out a crab, paused; then, pulling a long face, he said, "I can't."

Ke Yan laughed despite himself. "Then buy the dishes you can make."

"But that's too plain." Jiang Qi felt that since Auntie had asked him to cook, he had to show some sincerity.

Ke Yan followed behind Jiang Qi, picking out vegetables along with him.

Back home, Lan Ruyin rose from the bed as soon as she heard them leave. She opened their wardrobe: mostly suits and white shirts, followed by casual wear and loungewear, and then... pink, green, and blue T-shirts in every soft pastel shade. The colors and smaller sizes clearly belonged to Jiang Qi. She pulled out a pale-green shirt decorated with two simply drawn sheep.

Truly poor taste, Lan Ruyin thought, though she looked twice more before returning it. In a drawer, she found a photo album. It held only two or three pictures from each year, but they spanned a full seven years, from their university days to the present. Beside each was a note recording the time, place, and mood.

Lan Ruyin put the album back. Their feelings were not shallow at all. They ran very deep. But...

Jiang Qi and Ke Yan soon returned, and Jiang Qi pulled out all the stops to cook a tableful of dishes for Ke Yan's mother to taste.

At the table, Lan Ruyin looked at the dishes and was reasonably satisfied: a plate of stir-fried greens, a plate of stir-fried potato, a plate of red-braised ribs, and a pot of corn-and-rib soup.

Ke Yan picked up the tastiest dish, the shredded potato, for Lan Ruyin; she ate a bite — too salty, though the flavor was passable.

She tried a bite of the greens — overcooked to mush; took a sip of the corn-and-rib soup — too bland, only a faint taste of corn and meat; and finally tried the red-braised ribs — a burnt taste flooded her mouth at once. Just as she was about to spit it out, she saw Ke Yan take a piece of the ribs too; she wanted to stop him, but he'd already eaten it, and said nothing about it being awful.

Lan Ruyin was so startled she swallowed the meat in her mouth — had she just happened to pick the bad piece? So she took another piece — but it was just as awful.

Seeing Lan Ruyin keep taking from the red-braised ribs, Jiang Qi assumed she liked them, and, afraid there wouldn't be enough, didn't take from that plate himself — so Jiang Qi had no idea how badly the red-braised ribs had turned out.

Lan Ruyin struggled through the meal, and on her way out said one thing to Jiang Qi at the door: "From now on just leave the cooking to Auntie Li; come home from work and rest properly. Even if Ke Yan wants to eat your cooking, just have Auntie Li make it..."

What Lan Ruyin meant was simple: Jiang Qi cooked terribly, yet Ke Yan would eat it anyway. Better to leave dinner to Auntie Li before her son made himself ill.

Jiang Qi, however, heard concern that cooking after work would tire him out.

And so, this meeting left Jiang Qi quite happy — thank goodness Ke Yan's mother liked him.
