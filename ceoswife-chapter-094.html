Chapter 94

On the Matter of Adopting a Child 关于领养孩子

July 2026

~7 min read

"Where are you taking me, exactly?" Lin Zirui asked from the passenger seat.

"To my godson's first birthday party."

"Your godson. Why do I have to go?" Lin Zirui kept his eyes on the window.

"Because you're the godmother." Yang Shaoyu answered without missing a beat.

Lin Zirui said nothing more, watching the street slide past as though he hadn't heard a word. A long moment passed. "Why do you think that is?"

"Why is what?" The light had gone red; Yang Shaoyu eased the car to a stop and turned to look at him.

"Nothing." The reply came out flat and dry.

Why is it always when I've given up and turned away that you come back?

Courage was perhaps the one thing Lin Zirui had always lacked. He'd spent all of it chasing Yang Shaoyu the first time — spent it down to nothing — and now, when Yang Shaoyu was the one reaching out, he wasn't sure he had any left to reach back with.

Upstairs, Ke Xin was reading. In that, at least, Ke Yan took after his father almost exactly.

Lan Ruyin came in and dropped into a chair, out of sorts, her expression unusually grave. It was a rare thing to see her like this. Ke Xin set down his book and took off his glasses. "What's wrong?"

"Nothing. Your darling son is back." There was an edge to it.

"Jiang Qi?"

"How did you know?"

"You told me you'd met him, didn't you? Last time. A good kid." Ke Xin did not often praise anyone.

"Hmph. And how would you know he's a good kid." She paused. "Though — he's not bad, really. If only…" She shook her head and gave a frustrated tug at the hair she'd had done just yesterday.

Ke Xin hadn't seen his wife this wound up in a very long time. "What is it, really?"

"Nothing. I'm just not happy with this hairstyle." And with that she swept out of the study, off to have it redone.

Ke Xin watched her go. Hadn't she sat in front of the vanity half of last night, admiring that very style?

"Jiang Qi, it's time for Xiao Ke's milk." Auntie Li held out a bottle.

Jiang Qi took it. The moment Xiao Ke caught sight of the bottle in his hands he lit up. "Milk — milk!"

Jiang Qi blinked, delighted. "He can talk!"

"He's a year old. Of course he can talk." Ke Yan glanced over as Xiao Ke seized the bottle and began to drink.

The baby's little mouth worked at the nipple, one hand wrapped around the bottle, the other clamped tight around one of Jiang Qi's fingers. He'd settled his back against Jiang Qi and arranged himself into a comfortable position of his own choosing.

"He's really so cute." Jiang Qi tugged at Ke Yan's hand.

Ke Xin came downstairs to exactly this: Jiang Qi on the sofa with Xiao Ke nestled in his arms drinking his milk, Ke Yan beside them, watching the two of them.

Ke Xin raised an eyebrow. There was, unexpectedly, a sense of harmony to the scene.

Had Jiang Qi been able to see him just then, he'd have been delighted to discover that the way Ke Xin raised an eyebrow was almost identical to the way Ke Yan did.

"Jiang Qi. This is the first time I've ever seen Ke Yan bring a friend home." Ke Xin spoke up.

"Hello, sir." Jiang Qi got the greeting out quickly. He didn't rise — afraid that standing would jostle the milk-drinking Xiao Ke and make him choke — but he dipped his head respectfully toward Ke Yan's father all the same.

"Mm. Come to Grandpa." Ke Xin held out his arms to the baby.

"Glug, glug." Xiao Ke went on drinking, ignoring him entirely, and squirmed closer against Jiang Qi.

Refused, Ke Xin withdrew his arms without the slightest awkwardness. "He likes you, this one." This, to Jiang Qi.

"He's very sweet, Xiao Ke." Jiang Qi smiled.

"Come here, baby, let Mama hold you a while." Mid-feeding, Ke Shao swept in and scooped Xiao Ke away to show him off to her friends.

Jiang Qi watched, a little reluctant to let go, as the baby allowed himself to be carried off without a struggle.

"Go and rest a bit. The party won't start for a while yet." Ke Xin checked the time.

Jiang Qi nodded. Ke Yan, who hadn't said a word all this time, stood and led him up to his room.

Ke Yan's room here was much like the one in their own home, with a single difference — a section had been set aside as a study.

A cream-white desk held two or three books, a couple of photo frames, a lamp, a pen holder in cream and black. Behind it stood a wall of shelves, floor to ceiling.

Jiang Qi picked up one of the frames. Both photographs were of Ke Yan with the whole family — one taken in his university years, the other after Xiao Ke was born.

He ran his fingers over the frame. Ke Yan came up behind him and circled an arm around his waist. "A pity you're not in it."

"I'm right here." Jiang Qi pointed to the spot on the photographed Ke Yan's chest.

"Here?" Ke Yan drew Jiang Qi's hand to the place over his own heart.

"Always here." And he bent his head and kissed him.

Something fluttered in Jiang Qi's chest — sweet, brimming over, an excitement and a happiness he hadn't the words for.

Later they lay together on Ke Yan's bed. Jiang Qi glanced toward the window. Evening already. "Why hold the party so late — eight o'clock? Xiao Ke's already asleep."

"Richard's away on business. Eight is the earliest he can get back."

"Oh. …Ke Yan. What would you say to us adopting a child?"

Ke Yan, helpless, lowered his head to kiss Jiang Qi's brow. "Give it a couple more years. I haven't had my fill yet of just the two of us."

"Mm. All right." Jiang Qi buried his face against Ke Yan's chest. He'd agreed — but how were two men supposed to adopt a child? The thought dragged his mood down.

Ke Yan felt the shift at once. "What is it?" He tilted Jiang Qi's face up.

"How are two men like us supposed to adopt a child?"

"Don't worry about it. Leave it to me." And because Ke Yan said so, Jiang Qi let himself stop worrying.

Jiang Qi drowsed while Ke Yan considered the problem. He could arrange an adoption, but ideally they would take in a child only a few months old. An older child might struggle to bond with them, and Ke Yan could already imagine Jiang Qi's grief if that happened.

They stayed there until the room went dark, then tidied themselves a little and went down.

Below, the household was sitting down to dinner. Seeing the two of them come down, Ke Shao said, "Come, eat something now to tide you over — once the guests arrive there may not be time."

Coming down the stairs, Jiang Qi was startled to spot Lin Zirui seated beside Yang Shaoyu. Could it be that Doctor Lin and Shaoyu were together now?

He flicked Ke Yan a questioning glance.

No idea.

Ke Yan looked back at him.

Lan Ruyin caught the two of them exchanging glances, shot Ke Yan a glare, and let it go.

They'd eaten only a little when the guests did, indeed, begin to arrive.

The Ke family all went out to the garden — even Yang Shaoyu got dragged along.

Jiang Qi and Lin Zirui ended up on the garden swing together. Jiang Qi smiled at him. "Doctor Lin, it's been a while. How have you been?"

"Mm, very well. Just call me Zirui."

"Zirui, then. How are things with you and Shaoyu?" Jiang Qi found Lin Zirui easy to warm to.

Lin Zirui paused, then smiled too. "I don't know." Didn't know whether it was a good thing or a bad one — whether it was what he wanted.

Jiang Qi let the question drop and they fell into conversation, finding, to their mutual surprise, that they had a great deal to talk about.

Lin Zirui knew, too, that when fate came around you were meant to seize it — and yet he'd flinched away. He hoped Yang Shaoyu wouldn't simply give up and shrink back the way he had. That was the thought in his head just then; he was deeply at odds with himself. His brow furrowed, then smoothed again — because, not far off, Yang Shaoyu had thrown him a roguish little grin, as if to say, Aren't I handsome?

Lin Zirui nearly laughed in spite of himself, but held it in. He wasn't quite ready to forgive him yet.
