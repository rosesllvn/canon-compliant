Not So Bad After All? 也不错？

July 2026

~5 min read

"Don't say any more. I'm tired — I'm going to rest a while." Mother Jiang ignored Jiang Qi's words, claimed tiredness, and turned back to her room.

Jiang Qi's eyes dimmed. He sat down on the sofa. He wouldn't go back today; he'd stay until his mother was willing to speak to him.

Ke Yan sat beside him, keeping him company.

"Xiao Qi, shall we talk?" Jiang Bu glanced at the two of them.

"All right."

"Er — though…" Jiang Bu glanced at Ke Yan.

"Brother, it's fine, go ahead." Jiang Qi had no intention of stepping away from Ke Yan to talk.

"All right then." Jiang Bu sat on the adjacent sofa. "When did you two meet?"

"In university." Ke Yan answered.

The question Jiang Bu had been about to ask stalled — Ke Yan's presence was too commanding, and he wasn't quite used to it.

"Ahem — how did you meet?" Jiang Bu cleared his throat.

"Brother, remember how Mom went up the mountain for a protection charm when I was seriously ill? I always carried it, even to university. After so many years, it had come loose. One day, while I leaned on the dormitory railing, it fell—and Ke Yan caught it from the floor below. I went down to retrieve it from him." Even after all these years, Jiang Qi remembered their meeting clearly.

And so did Ke Yan — still remembered the dazed look on Jiang Qi's face that day.

"…" Jiang Bu hadn't expected that to be how they met. Was it great fate, or just great coincidence — and the one who'd brought them together was, in a way, their own mother.

"But — where's the charm now?" Father Jiang and Yun Hui had been listening from the living room, and at this he couldn't help asking.

"I was afraid of losing it, so I put it away."

"Where?"

"At home."

"At home — go get it, let me have a look." Father Jiang wanted to show it to Mother Jiang, to see how she'd react.

"Ah — it's not here." Jiang Qi caught himself.

"Didn't you say it's at home?" Yun Hui couldn't help saying; she wanted to see it too. Unable to accept the two of them at first, now, watching them, she found them rather well matched.

She found them well matched for two reasons: they genuinely were, and Jiang Lü had converted her with the BL manga and novels waiting in her room for another late-night reading session.

"It is at home — but at Ke Yan's and my home." Jiang Qi flushed saying it, a little embarrassed to say so in front of family.

"…" Jiang Bu and Father Jiang had the distinct sense of a son grown and gone — as if this house had become Jiang Qi's family home in the old sense, and Jiang Qi had brought his future husband to meet his in-laws.

Father Jiang sighed, struck by a sudden melancholy, as though he were marrying off a daughter.

"When in university did you two get together — exactly when?" Yun Hui couldn't contain the impulse to ask.

"Christmas." Jiang Qi blinked; it was Ke Yan who answered.

"Christmas? Isn't that almost here?" Yun Hui thought of how Christmas was only days off. If their mother still wouldn't agree, what then? It would ruin Christmas.

Well — it seemed eldest sister-in-law Yun Hui had been thoroughly converted into a fujoshi by Jiang Lü.

"Mm." Jiang Qi thought of the thing hidden in the study and glanced at Ke Yan, flushing despite himself.

It had been a long time since he'd felt this shy — perhaps because his family was right there; somehow it made Jiang Qi self-conscious.

Seeing Jiang Qi's bashful face, Father Jiang felt a pang — then glanced at Ke Yan and felt a little steadier. At least his son-in-law wasn't bad.

"I'll go check on your mom." Father Jiang stood, meaning to tell Mother Jiang how Jiang Qi and Ke Yan had met.

Mother Jiang had been sitting in the room staring into space, but it got too stifling, so she'd switched on the television — and then frozen, because she felt her stance had softened, and with it gone any mood to watch anything.

But what Father Jiang saw, coming in, was Mother Jiang watching television.

"In a good mood, are we?"

"Hmph. Where's Jiang Qi?"

"Still out there waiting for you to come out." Father Jiang sat down beside her.

She showed no reaction — but couldn't help wondering whether Jiang Qi had eaten.

"Do you know how those two met?"

"How did they meet?" She was a little curious too.

"When Jiang Qi was small, didn't he spend nearly half a month in the hospital? You went up the mountain to get him a protection charm."

"I did. Xiao Qi was so ill then." She remembered aching for him in those days.

So Father Jiang told her how Jiang Qi and Ke Yan had met. She listened, unsure what she felt — because it was the charm she'd gotten for Jiang Qi that had brought the two of them together?

"But — have you noticed? These past few years Jiang Qi has hardly been sick. No catching cold the moment the weather turns, no running to the hospital over every little thing."

At that, she realized Jiang Qi's health did seem better than before, illness rare.

"That's because Ke Yan takes care of him. You can see he's looked after Jiang Qi very well."

"Has he." She didn't argue.

Didn't Father Jiang know his wife? Seeing her like this now, her stance was plainly not so set against it as before.

This son-in-law wasn't bad after all, it seemed — at the very least, he'd taken good care of Jiang Qi.
