A Heart That Wouldn't Settle 心跳久久不能平复......

July 2026

~5 min read

Face still flushed from the kiss, Jiang Qi asked, "I thought you weren't back until the day after tomorrow. Why so early?"

"The contract came together, so I came back early."

"Then you'll be at the office late tonight?"

"Mm." Ke Yan considered it. "A little late, maybe — not too late. Wait up and come home with me?"

Jiang Qi turned away to change. "My mom asked me to come by tonight."

"Is that so. Two weeks I've held back, and you won't even indulge me a little." A sulky, wheedling note ran under the complaint.

Jiang Qi shot him a look and made for the stairs. "Come eat your breakfast." Shameless man, he thought — and felt the corners of his mouth tug up all on their own.

Ke Yan watched him go, his whole face softening.

After breakfast Jiang Qi stacked the bowls in the sink. The cleaning lady would come by later to tidy and restock the household odds and ends; since she didn't have to stay and cook, she was usually gone before noon. They shut the door behind them and walked to the elevator, where Ke Yan pressed the call button and turned. "Shall I drive you in?"

Jiang Qi shook his head. "Don't bother. Last time you dropped me off, Xiao An cornered me for ages afterward, wanting to know whether something was going on between us."

Xiao An — full name Ling Ling'an — was a clerk in Jiang Qi's department, hired the year before. Almost from her first day she'd fixed her sights on him: buying him breakfast, eating lunch with him, orbiting his desk from morning to night. Jiang Qi found it baffling. It wasn't that he thought her sweet on him — if anything her interest felt like curiosity, the way she watched him like a specimen under glass, until his skin prickled all over. That was how he read it, anyway. Ke Yan read it differently, and had sulked and stewed over it for a good while, which left Jiang Qi at his wit's end; most mornings he could scarcely drag himself out of bed.

After a week of it he finally reached his limit and asked Xiao An out for coffee after work.

"So, Ling'an—" He was still working out how to broach it.

"Just call me Xiao An — what's with the 'Ling'an,' honestly. Heh heh heh." She beamed, and tacked on a distinctly eerie little laugh.

Jiang Qi shivered in spite of himself, forced out a chuckle, and tried again. "Ling— ah, Xiao An. Do you have a boyfriend?" He sipped his coffee, thinking that if she didn't, he'd set her up with someone and be done with it. Any longer like this and Ke Yan was going to snap his back clean in two.

"What? What's that supposed to mean?" The shriek came so suddenly that Jiang Qi choked mid-sip. When he'd recovered he managed, "What is it?"

Realizing she'd lost her composure, Xiao An clapped a hand over her mouth, laughed twice — "heh heh" — then leaned in, low and tense. "Manager Jiang. Don't tell me you like me? Because that won't do at all."

"No — no." Afraid she'd misunderstood, he hurried to set her straight. "The truth is I wanted to introduce you to someone. Don't get the wrong idea."

"Oh. Good, then." Xiao An let out a breath. "You scared me half to death. Although — I'll say it — you do rather look the type."

The words landed in his chest with a small thud. "What would make you think that — ha, ha." He forced out two dry laughs and lifted his cup again.

Xiao An waggled her head, mysterious and knowing. "Call it the instinct of a seasoned fujoshi. Instinct."

Jiang Qi had no idea what a fujoshi was, but he didn't press. What mattered was that she plainly knew nothing about him and Ke Yan — so he made his hasty goodbyes and left.

Xiao An's temperament was a little like his younger sister's, easy to be around; and so, without quite knowing how, the two of them had become friends.

In the car, Jiang Qi buckled in and turned to watch Ke Yan drive. Two weeks apart, and though they'd spoken every day by phone, he'd missed him keenly. It was an awkward thing: when Ke Yan was away Jiang Qi thought of him constantly, yet now that the man sat right beside him, he wore a studied indifference.

Ke Yan glanced over. Jiang Qi's lips were still faintly swollen from the morning; the pale line of his profile, the shy set of his face, gave him an air of meekness that made Ke Yan's throat tighten. He pulled to the curb, took in the nape of that neck, and bent to it without restraint.

He bit gently at Jiang Qi's mouth, kissing from lips to chin, working the shirt buttons open as he went; at the curve of the throat he parted his lips and closed his teeth lightly over the skin. The suddenness of it startled Jiang Qi — but he had no wish to push him away. He hooked both arms around the back of Ke Yan's neck and let him lick and nip along his throat as he pleased.

At last Ke Yan drew back. The sight of Jiang Qi undone like this pulled a wicked little smile from him — and then he didn't dare look any longer, afraid he'd lose his hold and take Jiang Qi right there in the car. He set the clothes to rights, pressed a kiss to his forehead, and started the engine.

Jiang Qi sank into his seat, his heart a long way from settling.
