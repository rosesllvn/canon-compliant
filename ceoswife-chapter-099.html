Day One of Ke Yan's Trip — Jiang Qi Does Just Fine 柯言出差第一天江奇很好

July 2026

~6 min read

Yang Shaoyu had been at the hospital, waiting for Lin Zirui to finish his operation, when Ke Yan's call came through. He knew well enough how important the matter was. He glanced at the shut doors of the operating room, then went to the nurses' station and left word with the head nurse. "When Doctor Lin's out of surgery, tell him something's come up and I had to go. And tell him to make sure he eats."

"All right, I'll let him know. You're really very thoughtful — anyone who didn't know better would think Doctor Lin was your girlfriend."

Yang Shaoyu laughed. "He will be soon enough."

"Ah?" The head nurse felt sure she'd misheard.

"I'm off, then. I'll leave it to you." And he left.

Before heading to the office, Yang Shaoyu drove first to the restaurant Lin Zirui liked, ordered the dishes Lin Zirui was fond of, arranged for them to be delivered there every day, paid, settled the details — and only then hurried in to work.

Not long after, Lin Zirui came out, propping up his exhausted body, and felt a small pang of disappointment to find Yang Shaoyu gone from outside the operating room. But he composed himself quickly. Waiting in his office, perhaps?

Back in his office, no Yang Shaoyu. Lin Zirui sighed. Had he gone home?

"Doctor Lin?"

He turned. The head nurse stood in the doorway.

"Mr. Yang said something came up and he had to leave — and he asked me to remind you to eat."

"Really? Thank you." A small gladness rose in Lin Zirui at once.

"It's nothing. Remember to eat, now — I'll get back to it."

Lin Zirui rested a while, then decided to do as Yang Shaoyu said and have his meal first.

Passing the nurses' station, he was stopped. "What is it?" He looked at the young nurse who'd called out to him.

"Your takeout. Mr. Yang Shaoyu had it sent over."

"Oh." Lin Zirui took it.

Back in the office, he opened the containers. Yang Shaoyu really was very thoughtful.

Jiang Qi spent the night at the Ke house and missed Ke Yan beside him. The next morning, he worried that he would have to go home for clothes before work, but Uncle Fu brought him a new suit. It fit perfectly. Once washed and dressed, Jiang Qi went down to breakfast.

"Ke Yan's gone back. How did you sleep?" At the table, Lan Ruyin watched Jiang Qi sit down to breakfast in his suit.

"Since you're Ke Yan's friend, don't be a stranger. We rarely see him bring anyone home but Shaoyu and those two madcap girls," Ke Xin added.

"Mm, I understand. I slept very well, thank you." Jiang Qi smiled.

"Good." Ke Xin nodded.

"Drink up." Lan Ruyin set a glass of milk in front of him.

"Thank you, ma'am." Jiang Qi was quick with his thanks.

Jiang Qi's first breakfast alone at the Ke house, without Ke Yan there, went rather well.

Perhaps because of Ke Yan's parting words the night before, or perhaps because of an afternoon spent together, Lan Ruyin now found Jiang Qi quite agreeable — and when he left for work, she even told the driver to take it easy on the road.

Riding to the office, Jiang Qi looked up at the top floor and wondered what Ke Yan was doing now.

"How come we haven't seen the CEO or his assistant today?" Over lunch in the canteen, Jiang Qi overheard the staff talking.

"Off on a business trip, I heard."

"Oh, no wonder. Still — somehow without the CEO around I can't muster any drive to work."

"Tsk. As if you ever have any drive to work." Jiang Qi smiled, listening.

"What's funny?" Xiao An and Jiang Lü sat down to find him smiling.

"Just listening to them." Jiang Qi nodded back at the staff behind him.

"Oh — Ge, Mom says she's never seen your place. She's been saying for days she wants to come take a look." This from Jiang Lü.

"Cough — cough, cough." Jiang Qi choked and broke into a fit of coughing.

"What's wrong?" Xiao An passed him her unopened drink.

"Nothing."

"Nothing, but you jump out of your skin at what I said?" Jiang Lü eyed him, puzzled.

"You haven't got a little man stashed away at home, have you?" Xiao An joked idly.

"…" Guiltily, Jiang Qi dropped his head and ate.

Xiao An had only been teasing — never dreaming she'd hit the mark. There really was a man. Not a little one, though. Jiang Qi's man.

"How did Mom come to bring it up?" Jiang Qi asked.

"She was chatting with her friends. They were saying their sons' rooms are always a mess and they can't look after themselves." Jiang Lü sometimes sat in the living room and listened to their mother and her circle of friends talk.

"Oh." Jiang Qi resolved, privately, to head his mother off at all costs.

"Come to think of it, I've never seen this place you're renting either. I'm coming with Mom."

"Me too!" Xiao An cried, thrilled. "Maybe there really is a little man. Maybe there's even a CEO?" She'd always thought Jiang Qi and a CEO would be wonderful together.

"I'm done — you two carry on." Jiang Qi bolted the rest of his lunch and beat an undignified retreat.

What he dreaded now was his mother calling. If she came to his place and saw it, it would be far too easy to give away that he and Ke Yan lived together.

The flat was too big, too nice — there was no way his mother would believe he lived there alone.

And the place was full of things tied to him and Ke Yan: photo albums, matching couples' pajamas, clothes, even Ke Yan's documents.

In short, none of it could be allowed to meet his mother's eyes.

But Jiang Qi's hopes were dashed. Near the end of the workday his mother called — and, exactly as Jiang Lü had said, fretting that he couldn't take care of himself, insisted on coming to see for herself.

Jiang Qi said, helplessly, "I take perfectly good care of myself. And I've lived on my own this long — how could I not manage?"

"I just want a look. It's nothing."

"I'm busy these days. And don't worry, I look after myself fine."

"Hmph. Is it that you don't want me underfoot, making a mess of your place? Is that it? Tired of your old mother?"

"No — no, really, no, I don't think anything of the sort." Jiang Qi rushed to say.

"Good. Then I'll come by this Saturday." And she hung up.

Listening to the dial tone, Jiang Qi felt he'd walked straight into a trap of his mother's laying.

Nothing for it. It seemed he'd have to get certain things in the flat tidied away before Saturday.
