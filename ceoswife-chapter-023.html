A Heap of Sour Clothes 一堆酸衣服

July 2026

~5 min read

That afternoon Jiang Qi came back from class to find the dorm strewn with clothes and trousers, two suitcases flung wide, the underwear and socks and assorted belongings inside on full display. He froze a dozen-odd seconds, then shouted, "Ye Xingwei, where are you — get over here and clean up your clothes."

"I'm behind you."

The voice at his back gave him a start. He turned, grabbed Ye Xingwei by the arm, and hauled him in. "Hurry up and tidy your clothes away."

Ye Xingwei pulled over a chair, sat with a flourish, crossed his legs, and wore the face of a man with no shame whatsoever. "No. I don't know how."

"Don't know how?" Jiang Qi thought about it and supposed it made sense — a rich family; naturally he'd never done housework. "All right, then."

Then Jiang Qi said something baffling. "What?" Ye Xingwei asked.

"I'll teach you," Jiang Qi said brightly.

At that delighted look, Ye Xingwei had a powerful sense of foreboding — but his mouth said, "Sure."

Jiang Qi opened the wardrobe, and found it crammed with clothes balled into wads — and a faintly sour smell. His face went dark. You've got to be kidding. Don't tell me every set he changes out of just gets stuffed in here — no wonder I never see him hanging laundry; he doesn't even wash it. What a slob. Whatever rich-young-master image Ye Xingwei had held in his mind now collapsed utterly, without leaving so much as a crumb.

"Hey, how can you just leave your clothes unwashed — they're nearly growing mold."

Ye Xingwei covered his nose. "Throw them out, quick, they reek."

"Just wash them and you can wear them again — go on, go wash them."

"Oh. Fine."

Jiang Qi nodded, satisfied, and gathered up the clothes flung across bed and floor. When he turned back, Ye Xingwei had long since fled. He ground his teeth, fists clenched around the clothes, then helplessly set them on the bed and began folding.

Down on the floor below, Ye Xingwei glanced up toward the dorm and curled his lip. "As if — telling me to tidy up. Not a chance." Never minding who'd flung the clothes everywhere in the first place.

Should I wash them for him? Jiang Qi looked at the wardrobe of stinking clothes. But why should I? He just sneaked off too. Maybe I'll let them molder. But then they'd be unwearable — such a waste. He wavered a while — and then his eyes began to roll round and round, scheming.

He fetched an extra-large black bag, used one of Meng Qing's pens to shovel all of Ye Xingwei's clothes in, and tied it shut. Then he dialed Meng Qing. "Meng Qing, where are you? Get back to the dorm, quick."

"What's so important? Running back and forth, I'll die of the heat — just say it on the phone."

"If you want to make money, come now, or it'll be too late. And — you might just make a lot of money." He hung up, then sent word to Liu Hao the same way.

Meng Qing, thoroughly uninterested, twitched a brow at money; and at a lot of money his whole body came up off the desk, and he came racing back.

When the two arrived, what they found was an enormous black bag, and Jiang Qi standing in front of Ye Xingwei's wardrobe. Meng Qing rummaged in the bag. "Whoa — these are more designer than designer, some you couldn't even buy."

Liu Hao pulled one out and examined it. "This looks like Ye Xingwei's — saw him in it a couple of days ago."

"It is his. I dug them out of his wardrobe."

"Really?"

"Mm. Wash them clean and you could sell them online as secondhand — though I don't know whether they'd sell." Seeing Ye Xingwei didn't want them, Jiang Qi made the suggestion.

"Heh heh..." Meng Qing let out a sly little laugh, as though he'd thought of something, his glee plain on his face.

Jiang Qi and Liu Hao looked at the expression and couldn't help shaking their heads. What devious scheme has Meng Qing come up with now?

Jiang Qi checked the time — about time for the library. "I've got something to do, I'll go on ahead."

Liu Hao watched him go, thoughtful. "Jiang Qi seems to have something to do at this exact hour every day."

Jiang Qi could not in that moment have imagined what the two of them would go on to get up to.

That night Ye Xingwei came back to a clean, tidy dorm. Jiang Qi tidied up for me? He opened the wardrobe, and sure enough, the clothes were neatly folded and put away. He reached out and touched the white shirt on top, and the corner of his mouth tugged up. Since childhood, apart from his mother and the servants, no one had folded his clothes for him — not even his two strong-willed, capable older sisters. Hm? He took down a note pinned to the wardrobe:

Your personal items are in the suitcase — sort those yourself. Don't go flinging your clothes around anymore. I won't tidy up for you next time. — Jiang Qi

Ye Xingwei pulled out the suitcase and opened it: underwear and socks. Heh. He couldn't help a small laugh. This Jiang Qi was really... rather cute.
