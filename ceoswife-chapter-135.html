The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 135

Let's Get Married 我们结婚吧

July 2026

~10 min read

Christmas. Decorations everywhere — malls, streets, plazas, the company — Christmas trees large and small all over.

Christmas fell on a Friday, so the company remained open. Those with partners could not go on dates; those without barely cared.

For Jiang Qi and Ke Yan, Christmas marked the anniversary of their confession. Today, Jiang Qi retrieved the hidden ring from the study. He meant to propose.

He wondered whether Ke Yan would be startled. He opened the ring box: inside, a very plain platinum band, engraved. When he'd gone to choose it, this was the only one that caught his eye — simple, and Ke Yan liked simple things.

"You'll love the Christmas gift I'm giving you this year," Ke Yan said as they reached the company, just before Jiang Qi got off the elevator.

"What is it?" Jiang Qi was curious; Ke Yan gave him gifts every year, but had never said "you'll love it" like this.

"You'll see this afternoon." Ke Yan only smiled.

"All right then." Jiang Qi got off.

The elevator closed again.

"…"

It was very quiet inside. Lately, as always, Ke Yan skipped his private elevator and rode the staff one up with Jiang Qi — but ordinarily no one dared crowd in.

Today, though, the elevator was far from empty; some people even rode past their own floors without getting off.

They stood to the side, sneaking looks at Manager Jiang and the CEO — though even standing aside, they kept a full person's distance from Ke Yan.

No one dared touch the CEO, or jostle their way close to him.

Ke Yan didn't mind being watched with Jiang Qi, and said nothing.

But everyone noticed: the moment Manager Jiang got off, the CEO's temperature "dropped."

Wang Siqi had wanted to find Sun Nianqing yesterday, to get her thinking of a plan, but Sun Nianqing had been out sick, and she didn't want to ask the secretary for her number, so she'd had to wait until today.

But arriving at the office that morning, she found several people clustered together, talking about how tender the CEO had been toward Manager Jiang in the elevator, and saying something about a Christmas gift.

Wang Siqi seethed. Why, on a perfectly good Christmas, did she have to hear about how good Ke Yan was to Jiang Qi?

"Work." Her mood foul, she nearly shouted the word, her voice cracking on it.

At that everyone scattered to their stations and got to work, not a word of gossip more.

It was Christmas, and gossiping had nearly gotten them reprimanded — best just work fast; once off, they could go on their dates.

So the morning passed, and at noon Wang Siqi went to find Sun Nianqing.

The moment Sun Nianqing saw her coming, she knew what it was about — she'd heard plenty of gossip in half a morning herself — so before Wang Siqi could speak, she said, "I know what you want to say. I don't believe their feelings can run that deep. I won't let it go at this." And she left her seat for the canteen.

Wang Siqi stared after her, blank. What did Sun Nianqing take her for? Really! She left too — though the clack of her heels made plain how angry she was.

After lunch, Ke Yan said he wanted to take Jiang Qi somewhere.

"Where?"

"To look across from the company." Ke Yan brought Jiang Qi to the company's middle floor.

"Is there something over there?" Across the way, apart from the two shops being renovated — wrapped in cloth so no one could see inside — everything looked as usual.

"Look closer."

Jiang Qi looked closer — and noticed: the cloth wrapping the shop was red, printed with Santa Claus, so that from afar it looked like a giant gift box.

"Ke Yan, what's that? Is it a present?" Jiang Qi grabbed his hand.

"It is. Come, I'll take you to see." Ke Yan clasped his hand back and led him across.

"What is it?" Jiang Qi was curious — a gift that big?

"You'll see in a moment."

They reached the spot, standing before the enormous present, and Ke Yan took a cord from it and handed it to Jiang Qi. "Pull it."

Jiang Qi gave it a firm pull, and the whole cloth came loose and slid to the ground. Before him appeared a shop: green outer walls, an arched wooden door trimmed with green leaves, the green walls painted with a mural — a forest rendered in every shade.

Looking up, the signboard was made of wood too, carved with — "Jiang Qi's Tree Nook."

Jiang Qi stepped up and touched the door handle, eyes wet, and pressed his forehead to the door.

"What's wrong?" Ke Yan put an arm around his shoulders; he'd taken in every flicker of Jiang Qi's expression, terrified he wouldn't like the design.

"Nothing." Jiang Qi shook his head, but the tears were nearly falling.

Ke Yan wiped them for him. "Why are you crying, silly?"

"Because I'm so moved. Ke Yan — if there's a next life, I want to meet you again, be with you again. No one is better to me than you, no one loves me more. I never imagined you'd give me this." Jiang Qi thought of the wish Ke Yan had heard before and kept in his heart, quietly making it come true.

"If I'm not good to you, I'd be good to no one. Jiang Qi, I love you — I really do love you. I love spoiling you, love seeing you smile. Your wishes, I'll do everything I can to make come true, only to see you smile. Don't cry — come, let's go in."

Ke Yan took Jiang Qi's hand to the handle, and together they pushed the door open.

Inside, the palette was nearly all green too — but what caught Jiang Qi's eye and held it was one wall Ke Yan had left bare, painted with the two of them as chibi figures, the whole story of how they'd met and fallen in love, the final panel their confession under the Christmas tree.

Jiang Qi smiled and went to touch the little figures on the wall. "So cute."

"Come look over here." Ke Yan led him up to the little attic above.

"I had it built specially during the renovation. And I owe you one more gift."

"What gift?"

"A child. In a few days we'll buy furniture and fit this out, so when you pick the child up from school, the child can rest here in this room and wait for me to get off work, and we'll go home together."

"All right — and remember, you still owe me a gift." Jiang Qi wasn't sad anymore; on the contrary, his mood was wonderful.

"This shop has two floors for shelving books, with spaces set out for customers to read. I've partitioned the areas, but some still need your say-so before they're finished." Ke Yan walked him through both floors.

"All right — but I'd like an area set aside just for children to read." Jiang Qi had been to many bookshops that sold children's books but had no dedicated children's space, so the little ones had to sit among the adults — and some adults didn't like children nearby, leaving many kids to read on the floor.

"Done — though we can finish the shop tomorrow. Today I have another place to take you."

"Hm? Where? I wanted to take you somewhere too, though it's rather far." Jiang Qi checked the time, worried two places wouldn't fit.

"You want to take me to the Christmas tree at the plaza where we confessed?"

"How did you know?"

"Because I want to take you there too." The two smiled at each other, left the shop, got the car from the garage, and drove toward the plaza where they'd confessed.

In the car, Jiang Qi asked, curious, "Why name the shop Jiang Qi's Tree Nook?"

"First, 'Jiang Qi' is you—the name I most wanted on it. 'Tree' sounds like 'book,' and 'nook' reminds me how you curl up anywhere—bed, sofa, even the floor—to read. So I had to use it." [The shop's name puns on shù "tree" and shū "book," and on the image of Jiang Qi nestled up reading.]

"It's lovely." Jiang Qi loved it; the happiness in his chest nearly spilled over.

Yang Shaoyu, for his part, had spent the whole day on something important: arranging the furniture he'd bought into his home, having the workers place it to his specifications in the empty rooms, helping out while watching the clock — he should have it all swapped in before he left to pick up Lin Zirui.

Luckily Yang Shaoyu's place was big enough; otherwise there'd have been no room for so much furniture.

And just then Lin Zirui was clocking off — he'd deliberately told Yang Shaoyu a later time, so he could swing home, fetch some things, and surprise Yang Shaoyu.

He loaded box after box into the car, checked nothing was missing, and drove to Yang Shaoyu's place — no, he should say, home.

He had a warm home now, with Shaoyu in it. Lin Zirui smiled, happy.

At last he arrived, and looking at the boxes in the trunk, wondered how to get them up.

As luck had it, the complex security guard came over to ask if he needed help, and Lin Zirui asked him to help carry them up.

He unlocked the door, and with the guard's help got the boxes inside, then thanked him.

After shutting the door, he discovered that all the furniture had changed—to the pieces he and Shaoyu had chosen together. The supposed friend had been an excuse for this surprise. Lin Zirui was deeply moved.

Suddenly footsteps came from the stairs.

Yang Shaoyu came down to find Lin Zirui standing in the doorway.

He checked the time. "Why are you off early? Why didn't you call me to pick you up?"

"Shaoyu, thank you." Lin Zirui went and hugged him.

"Don't thank me. I wanted to give you a warm home, furnished with things you like — a home for the two of us." Yang Shaoyu knew what gift Lin Zirui treasured most: a home that was theirs together. The thought alone made him happy.

"All right, I won't say thank you. Oh — I bought some things; let's set them out, shall we?"

"What things?"

Yang Shaoyu watched Lin Zirui open the boxes — everything inside in matching pairs.

Together they took the things out: two pairs of men's couple's slippers, toothbrushes, pillows, cups, all sorts of things.

His eyes full of tenderness, Yang Shaoyu sat on the floor with Lin Zirui, taking the things out and placing them around the home.

This was their home, a warm home — his and Zirui's home.

"Zirui, this is our home, forever."

"Yes. We'll be together forever, always, in this warm home." Lin Zirui held him tight. As a child he'd had no parents to cherish him — but it didn't matter; now he had a partner who cherished him, Yang Shaoyu, and a warm home, his and Shaoyu's.

Meanwhile, by the time Ke Yan and Jiang Qi reached the plaza where they'd once confessed, it had gone dark.

But the colored lights on the Christmas tree lit the plaza bright.

For the first time, in a place this crowded, Jiang Qi was about to do something momentous, and happy — propose to Ke Yan. He took out the ring box, dropped to one knee. "Ke Yan, I love you. Let's get married."

Ke Yan smiled, took out a ring too, and knelt likewise, opening his box. "Jiang Qi, I love you too. Let's get married."

"Yes." Jiang Qi let Ke Yan slide the ring onto his finger.

Then Jiang Qi slid one onto Ke Yan's.

The same place, the same two people, the same Christmas tree. Seven years had carried them from confession to proposal, through so much, while their loving hearts remained unchanged.

Today, heedless of the strange looks around them, they bravely knelt before the Christmas tree and proposed to each other.

And the happiness would go on — they would be happy, always.
