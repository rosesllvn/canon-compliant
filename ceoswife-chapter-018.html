Chapter 18

This Person Has No Heart 这人真没爱心

July 2026

~4 min read

Meanwhile, crouched at the window peeping in, Meng Qing and Liu Hao felt the rain start. Liu Hao shielded his head with one hand. "Let's head back."

"All right — we've investigated about enough anyway." Meng Qing fished a folding umbrella from his back pocket and opened it. "Good thing I came prepared."

Liu Hao looked at him with admiration, deciding he really was cut out for this line of work.

Because of the umbrella, and for fear the Ke Yan inside would spot them, they had to creep forward in a squat, step by step. When they finally reached the front of the house they stood and stretched; that crouch-walk had been thoroughly miserable.

Liu Hao looked around but didn't find Jiang Qi. "Strange — where'd he go?"

"He couldn't have gone back, could he?"

"Surely not — knowing Jiang Qi, he'd have told us."

"Then... he couldn't have been caught, could he?" Meng Qing guessed, and on reflection found it all too plausible. Liu Hao thought the odds high too. So they closed the umbrella, looked in through the living-room window, and saw Jiang Qi and Ke Yan sitting on the sofa — though the rain blurred their faces past reading. The two quietly backed away and put the umbrella up again. After a silence Liu Hao spoke. "He should be fine, ha ha." He forced out two dry laughs.

"Probably. So shall we...?"

"Head off first...?"

"Right, let's go." And the two left in indecent haste, leaving Jiang Qi alone to face the Ke Yan who, in their eyes, was very handsome but very terrifying.

Had Jiang Qi known what they were thinking just then, he'd surely have wished he could tie the pair to a big tree and leave them there to wait for the lightning.

On the sofa, Jiang Qi felt thoroughly ill at ease, as though a dozen needles were pointed up at him from under the seat, ready to jab at any moment.

Ke Yan watched his nervous discomfort and was seized by a sudden urge to tease him. He produced something from behind his back. "Is this yours?"

"Ah, that—" It's mine. Jiang Qi swallowed the words back down by main force. He looked at the camera — the one he'd flung into the bushes — and had no idea how to answer.

"Hm?"

"That... I don't know."

Ke Yan raised an eyebrow, opened the camera, and found photos of himself inside. "Since there are pictures of me in it, then it's not yours?"

Jiang Qi stopped answering. The camera really wasn't his — but if it got lost, wouldn't he have to pay for it?

Seeing he wouldn't answer, Ke Yan didn't press. He went to the window and looked out: the rain hadn't let up in the least, if anything coming down harder, thunder rolling with it. Someone else might have made the polite gesture of offering Jiang Qi a bed for the night — but not Ke Yan. He disliked strangers in his home; that he'd let Jiang Qi in at all was already a rarity.

"There's an umbrella in the shoe cabinet," he said evenly.

Jiang Qi understood the unspoken meaning: he was being driven out. Umbrella in hand, he stood in the doorway looking at the pelting downpour, then back at the shut door behind him. With difficulty he opened the umbrella and started forward — the wind came howling, driving rain all over his face. He reached up to wipe it away; a flash of white light, and a thunderclap hard on its heels, the crack of it startling him back, afraid he'd be struck. He hesitated, then pressed Ke Yan's doorbell. A good while passed with no one coming; he pressed a few more times, and at last Ke Yan opened the door.

Jiang Qi thickened his skin. "Um — could you let me come in first, and leave once the rain lets up a bit?"

"No." Ke Yan refused without a moment's thought, and shut the door with a bang.

Jiang Qi wrinkled his nose. This person hasn't an ounce of heart. In weather like this, how am I supposed to get back to school? — and here he'd entirely forgotten where Liu Hao and Meng Qing had got to. Helpless, he put the umbrella up, pitiful, and huddled before Ke Yan's door, waiting for the thunder to stop.

Ke Yan came out from his shower to drink some water, looked at the worsening weather, and thought: that fellow's gone back by now, surely? He opened the door — only to find Jiang Qi still there, looking at him in bedraggled, pitiful appeal. Ke Yan, who had next to no soft-heartedness in him, felt something go soft at the sight, and pulled him into the house.

If that "she-devil" and that "toilet" had seen this scene, they'd surely have laughed their teeth right out.
