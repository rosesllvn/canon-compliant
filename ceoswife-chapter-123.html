Ke Yan's Grand Charm Offensive 柯言的讨好大计

July 2026

~5 min read

Once inside, Jiang Qi set down the heavy box in his arms. He still had no idea what it contained.

"Here, have some water — or would you prefer tea, or coffee?" Mother Jiang poured Ke Yan a glass of water first, then, worried he mightn't like water, asked.

"Auntie, no need to stand on ceremony with me. Jiang Qi and I are such good friends — I feel close to you too."

"Good, good." She smiled. This Ke Yan really was a likable boy.

"Oh — Auntie, I brought some gifts for you and Uncle."

"You shouldn't have gone to such expense." She scolded him for it, but with a smile on her face.

"It's no expense — just a small token of my regard."

"Well, if you put it that way, I'll accept them." Mother Jiang wasn't one to feel awkward about gifts — since they'd been brought, she wouldn't make him take them back.

She'd watched Ke Yan carry in a dozen-odd bags and assumed tea, or scarves, something of that sort.

She picked up the bag beside her, expecting it light, and found it surprisingly heavy — couldn't lift it the first try.

She tried again with more force and heaved the bag onto the table. "What's in here that's so heavy?" she asked, tearing open the wrapping.

"Jiang Qi said you like these, so I bought them. I hope you like it."

She wrestled the wrapping off, puzzled at the foam padding — and opening it, found a dinnerware set.

"How exquisite." She looked at the plates, truly lovely. "But how did you decide this was what I'd like best?" She glanced at Jiang Qi, who was watching television beside Ke Yan.

"Didn't you used to say, when people gave gifts, that pots and pans would be better?" said Jiang Qi, at last noticed by his mother.

"I don't like it when people give me tea — only your father does, that's why I said I'd rather have cookware. But this dinnerware Ke Yan brought really is beautiful." This son of hers really didn't understand her.

"As long as you like it, Auntie, I'm relieved." Ke Yan smiled.

"I do, it's very pretty."

Curious, she opened another bag and found an elegant box from a familiar brand. She had long wanted one of its scarves but could never bring herself to spend the money. Receiving one delighted her.

Still — "Isn't this too much expense?" She felt a little bad about the cost.

"Not at all. If you like the gifts I bring, no expense is too much. And I've called you Auntie — how could it be an expense? It's just a small token of my regard."

Listening from the side, Jiang Qi couldn't help glancing at Ke Yan. Nothing but charming words today.

Mother Jiang thought Ke Yan had marvelous taste — two items in and she already loved them, never mind how much he'd brought.

"Mom, come look, the fish is getting a bit scorched." Yun Hui — Jiang Qi's eldest sister-in-law — came out of the kitchen in an apron.

"Coming. I'll go cook, then," she told Ke Yan.

"Of course, Auntie, don't let me keep you. Your cooking really is delicious."

At that, she beamed wider still.

She checked the ingredients in the kitchen — enough — and decided to make a few more of her signature dishes.

With his mother in the kitchen, Jiang Qi leaned close to Ke Yan and murmured, "I had no idea you could talk like that."

"One has to charm one's mother-in-law."

While they talked, neither noticed Jiang Lü snap a photo of them leaning together.

Then, not daring to go up and talk to the CEO and her brother, she went back to her room and immediately sent the photo to Xiao An.

Jiang Qi and Ke Yan watched television in the living room, the clatter of Mother Jiang and Yun Hui cooking drifting from the kitchen.

Suddenly the door sounded; both turned to look. Father Jiang was home.

"Uncle." Ke Yan rose quickly at the sight of him.

"You must be Ke Yan?"

"Yes."

"Very handsome — far better-looking than Jiang Qi."

Jiang Qi shot his father a silent look.

"Come, sit, don't stand on my account." Father Jiang waved Ke Yan back down.

"Uncle, I heard you like chess, so I have a gift for you."

"Oh? What gift?" Father Jiang's interest perked up.

Ke Yan opened the box Jiang Qi had set aside, revealing a board and pieces.

Jiang Qi saw it and thought how thoughtful Ke Yan was — his father would surely love it.

Sure enough, Father Jiang smiled. "I'd never have guessed you'd bring me a chess set — no one's ever given me one. But Ke Yan, do you play? Let's have a game or two."

"I do — I've only just learned. I'd be glad of your guidance, Uncle."

"All right, let's start now." Father Jiang was delighted a young man would take to chess; none of his own children cared for it.

Ke Yan and Father Jiang began to play, and Jiang Qi watched television alone — everyone seemed to have forgotten him. But Jiang Qi smiled. As long as his family liked Ke Yan, that was good — and if only they could still feel this way after learning the nature of his and Ke Yan's relationship.
