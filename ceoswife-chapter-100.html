Jiang Qi's Mom Comes Over 江妈来了上

July 2026

~7 min read

Since his mother was coming Saturday, Jiang Qi had time enough to prepare and wasn't in any rush.

Home from work, he had dinner, read for a bit, then lost the appetite for reading and took a good hot shower before bed.

Burrowing under the covers, he gave a shiver. It had been getting colder and colder lately, and without Ke Yan to warm the bed for him, the moments before sleep were genuinely cold.

He rubbed his feet together, hugged the quilt close, and rolled about on the mattress. The bed felt vast — too big, somehow. He never noticed it with Ke Yan there. Now it felt far too large.

A cold draft seemed to keep blowing at his neck. He pulled the covers up higher and wrapped himself in tight.

Hurry home, Ke Yan.

Jiang Qi woke very early the next day, and not long after waking, Ke Yan called.

"Awake?"

"Mm, just now. It's the middle of the night where you are, isn't it — why are you up so early?"

"Didn't sleep all night. Figured you'd be up by now, so I called." Ke Yan rubbed wearily at the corner of his eye.

"That busy? You'll wear yourself out." Jiang Qi's heart ached for him.

"It's fine. I should be back by Monday. Cold at night when you sleep?"

"No. Not in the least."

"Learned to lie to me now, have you?"

"No." Jiang Qi's voice dropped at once, small and meek.

"Wear something warm to work today. The temperature's very low," Ke Yan instructed.

"How do you know it's cold here?" Jiang Qi asked.

"Checked the forecast. Figured you wouldn't. What if you went off to work in something thin and caught a chill, got sick?"

Warmth bloomed in Jiang Qi's chest. "I understand. Now — you mind yourself too. Wear something warm, don't work too hard, remember to eat, don't drink too much, and don't go chatting up any women."

That last line surprised a laugh out of Ke Yan. They talked a while longer before hanging up, reluctant on both sides.

"Manager Jiang? You're in a bit late today." Running into Sun Nianqing in the elevator, he was greeted with a smile.

Jiang Qi nodded, privately wondering — why was Sun Nianqing always smiling whenever he saw him?

The elevator arrived. Jiang Qi walked into his office, took in the desk piled with documents, and felt a small headache coming on. He really had gone soft lately. Looked like he ought to retire early, he thought, laughing at himself.

As it happened, he wasn't wrong — he would, in fact, retire decades early. Though he'd still be leaving the house with Ke Yan, and coming home with him, every day.

The days of Ke Yan's trip seemed to crawl by, but crawl by they did, and Thursday came. These past few days Jiang Qi had had the nagging sense he'd forgotten something.

He clocked off as usual and was about to head home for dinner when Jiang Lü stopped him. "Ge, can I come with Mom to your place the day after tomorrow?"

"Hm?" It didn't register at first.

"Ge, don't tell me you forgot. Didn't Mom already say she'd come to yours this Saturday?"

"Right." Jiang Qi went pale. It came back to him now, all of it.

"I can't believe how bad your memory is."

He nodded vaguely a few times and hurried off.

Jiang Lü watched him go. Why so flustered? Was there some secret at home that couldn't be found out?

It must be said — Jiang Lü had hit on the truth.

Jiang Qi reached home and stared around the large apartment, unsure where to begin. Finally, he hid every photo of himself with Ke Yan, separated their matching pajamas and clothes, cleaned the guest room, and moved some of his belongings into its wardrobe. Then he sat on the guest bed. Why exhaust himself like this? Telling Mom the truth would be easier—except Ke Yan wasn't home.

To Jiang Qi's mind, facing your parents was something the two of you ought to do together.

Because the two of you were going to share a life — that was why you met the parents at all. Whether they approved or not, you faced it together, and bore it together.

He knew, too, that not every love lasts a lifetime. But he and Ke Yan would. He was certain of it.

Drowsy, Jiang Qi lay down on the guest-room bed, pulled up the covers, and fell asleep.

Waking the next morning, he was disoriented for a moment. Where was this? Then it came back — he'd fallen asleep in the guest room last night.

"Ugh. Time to get up for work." He lay there looking at the ceiling, and only after a while did he get up. Getting up in winter was wretched.

He fetched clothes from the master bedroom, washed up, ate breakfast, and went to work.

He yawned. Sleeping in the guest room was, somehow, not very comfortable.

Saturday arrived quickly. The night before, Jiang Qi hadn't done any more tidying — he figured it was enough just to have put away the things that were too plainly his and Ke Yan's.

There was no chance his mother would believe he lived in a flat this size alone, so Jiang Qi decided to tell her he shared it with someone.

Thank goodness Ke Yan wasn't back until Monday — to walk in on Jiang Qi's mother would have given him a fright.

Heading out to fetch her, Jiang Qi cast a last look over the flat. It should be fine now.

He picked up his mother, and Jiang Lü had come along for the fun of it too.

The three of them walked into the complex. Jiang Lü looked around. "Wow, Er-ge, how can you afford to live in a place this upscale? Doesn't this complex not even rent units out? The flats here sell for a fortune."

"Do they?" Jiang Qi had genuinely forgotten about that. "Uh — this is a friend's place. I pay him rent every month."

"Oh — then your friend must be loaded, Er-ge."

"It's good to live somewhere nice — should be very safe here," his mother said, nodding.

"Here we are. Come in." Jiang Qi opened the door and let them through.

"Wow, it really is big," said Jiang Lü.

"How much is a month's rent on this?" His mother took in the spacious flat.

"Mm — not much. Because he's a friend, it's only two thousand a month."

"How is it I've never heard you mention a friend like this?" His mother looked at him.

"Oh — I must've forgotten to bring him up." Again the guilt crept in.

"It's a lovely place." His mother and Jiang Lü settled onto the sofa; Jiang Qi poured them each a glass of water.

"And tidy, too. I thought I'd be coming over for a deep clean today."

His mother nodded, surprised the flat was so clean.

"See? I told you I take good care of myself."

"Mm. Hm? What's this?" His mother had felt something beside the cushion. She lifted it and found a box, picked it up—

Jiang Qi recognized the box in his mother's hand and his heart lurched. "Don't open it!" he cried at once.

Too late. She'd already opened it.

Disaster, Jiang Qi wailed inwardly. How could he have forgotten that.

His mother and Jiang Lü stared at the condoms in the box, frozen.

Jiang Qi said nothing either, having no idea what to say.

Why was something like that there? Because of one time before — the two of them on the sofa, suddenly in the mood, Ke Yan pressing him down right there, and Jiang Qi, worried about the sofa, telling Ke Yan to use a condom — and afterward neither of them had remembered to put it away.

This was all Ke Yan's fault, insisting on the sofa. Now look at the mess.

Jiang Qi had entirely forgotten it was he who'd insisted on the condom.

"Uh — that, that's not mine," he stammered.

"Then whose is it?" His mother closed the box again.

"It's my friend's. Yeah — his. He, uh — must've forgotten to put it away."

"Really?"

"Really, he's the one who uses them." Which wasn't even a lie — wasn't Ke Yan the only one who did?

"Oh. Well, Xiao Qi — if you've got a girlfriend, just say so. I wouldn't object."

"I know. But I don't have a girlfriend."

"Er-ge, take us up to see your room," Jiang Lü proposed.

"Sure."

Jiang Qi led the two upstairs, privately counting his blessings. Dodged that one.
