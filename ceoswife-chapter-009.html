The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 9

Dinner? Or Poison? 晚餐？毒药？

July 2026

~3 min read

That Saturday neither of them had work, and the two sat together on the sofa watching a film. Nestled in Ke Yan's arms, Jiang Qi asked, "What should we have for lunch?"

"Whatever you can make, we'll eat," Ke Yan said helplessly — because Jiang Qi could make exactly two dishes, stir-fried cabbage and shredded potato, and those, with the side dishes his mother sent over, were what the pair lived on at home.

Jiang Qi sat up out of his arms. "If those are the only two things I can make, won't you get sick of them?"

"No."

He said it, but Jiang Qi gave the matter serious thought, and then announced, "I'm going to my mom's." He made to leave at once; Ke Yan caught him. "What's wrong?"

"I'm going to learn to cook." And he pried the hand loose, ran to the door, threw on his shoes, and dashed out.

At his parents' he found his mother home for once, not off gossiping somewhere. She looked at her son, come running back out of nowhere. "What is it? Back so soon?"

"Er — I want to learn to cook," he said, ill at ease.

"What's brought this on?" his mother said, astonished. From the side Yun Hui put in: "Don't tell me he's got a girlfriend — wants to cook for her?"

His mother lit up. "Is that true, son?"

"Don't go guessing wild — I'm cooking for myself. There's no girlfriend."

"Listen to you. Stop thinking about work all the time; when you've a spare moment you should be finding a girlfriend, or one day no girl will have you." His mother grumbled on. I was claimed by Ke Yan long ago, Jiang Qi muttered inwardly — not that he dared say it, and if he did, never mind cooking lessons, his mother would more likely put poison straight in the food. "All right, all right, Mom, I've got it — hurry up and teach me."

"Come on, then." There was no doing anything with him.

They went into the kitchen and tied on aprons. His mother took a rack of pork ribs from the fridge and set it on the board. "Today Mom'll teach you to cook meat."

"All right."

"Sweet-and-sour, then."

"No — some other way." Ke Yan didn't like sweet things, so sweet-and-sour ribs were out.

"Red-braised, then."

"Mm, all right."

She handed him a cleaver. "Cut them into small pieces."

Cleaver in hand, Jiang Qi had no idea where to begin. He'd never once cut meat in his life!!

Jiang Lü came home and caught a whiff of something scorched the moment she stepped in. "Sis, what's that smell? Something burning?" she asked Yun Hui, lounging on the sofa.

Yun Hui said nothing, only gestured toward the kitchen. Jiang Lü went to look, then quietly retreated and sat back down to watch TV.

Jiang Qi stopped at a supermarket on the way home, so it was evening when he arrived. He went straight to the kitchen and started dinner. Ke Yan, ignored, followed him in and leaned against the doorframe — and watching the flustered scramble of hands and feet, couldn't help a private flicker of dread for the meal that awaited him tonight.
