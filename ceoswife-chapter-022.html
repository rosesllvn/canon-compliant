Chapter 22

The Two People in the Library's Far Corner 图书馆最角落的两个人

July 2026

~4 min read

Military training ended, and Jiang Qi felt he could finally breathe again; the moment he had no class, he all but ran for the library. He'd loved to read since childhood — not textbooks, of course, but all sorts of curious, interesting books, though what Jiang Qi found interesting were, as a rule, books almost no one else had ever read.

Toward evening the heat still hadn't broken, not a breath of wind, stifling beyond endurance. At this hour most people were in the canteen or holed up with the air-conditioning on, so in the library, apart from the attendant, only Jiang Qi remained.

At a window table in the farthest corner he lay slumped over the desk, head pillowed on his arm, fast asleep and sweetly so. An open book had toppled beside him, an unopened bottle of water next to it. The setting sun slanted in across his face; even in sleep the glare reached him, and he raised a hand to shield his eyes. Before long the hand slid down, and he frowned in discomfort.

Ke Yan came along with a bottle of water in one hand and a book in the other, and looked at the Jiang Qi asleep over the desk. Familiar. He was about to take a different seat — then placed him, and sat down across from him instead, opened his book, and began to read.

He came to this farthest corner at this same hour every day, because it was when the fewest people were about, and even those few never passed this spot — quiet, no one to disturb him. Ordinarily Jiang Qi would have left the library by now; only today he'd fallen asleep, and so the two had met.

As he read, Ke Yan noticed Jiang Qi shifting his head about, far from settled. He looked at the furrowed brow, and at the evening sun on that face, and after a silent moment picked up the book lying open on the desk and laid it gently over Jiang Qi's face.

The glare gone, the knotted brow smoothed, and Jiang Qi slept more peacefully.

After who knew how long, his head stirred and the book slid off. He lifted it, yawned, rubbed his aching neck — and started to find Ke Yan across from him. Quite naturally, he greeted him. "Ke Yan, you've come to read too." In his mind, though they'd spoken only a handful of times, they were fairly well acquainted by now.

"Mm."

Jiang Qi yawned again, picked up the water beside him, and took a big mouthful — then looked up to find Ke Yan watching. "Is something the matter?"

Ke Yan pointed at the bottle. "You're drinking mine."

"Huh?" Jiang Qi looked: he'd had only the one mouthful, yet it was half empty — Ke Yan had plainly drunk from it. His face went red. He passed over his own. "This one's unopened — here."

Ke Yan took it, closed the book he'd finished, and stood. Jiang Qi hurriedly stood too, and put the books away with him.

Walking out, Jiang Qi glanced at him. "Shall we go eat together?" And looked at him expectantly.

Ke Yan didn't look over, but felt the gaze all the same. "All right."

Jiang Qi broke into a smile and walked with him toward the canteen.

The canteen had thinned out. They finished and headed back, Ke Yan's dorm on the fourth floor, Jiang Qi's on the fifth; at the fourth Jiang Qi caught at his clothes. "My name's Jiang Qi."

Ke Yan didn't answer, only kept walking. Jiang Qi looked at the hand that had just clutched his clothes. He'll remember it, surely.

The next day Jiang Qi came to the library toward evening, sat in the farthest corner, and read. It was very quiet, nearly everyone gone. Before long, footsteps. He looked up — Ke Yan.

Seeing Jiang Qi have no particular reaction, Ke Yan sat across from him as before and began to read.

Reading together with Ke Yan, Jiang Qi thought, felt good. Comfortable. A faint, quiet ease spread between them, and so they read on in silence — not speaking, and yet it felt very good.
