Chapter 136

How to Meet the Parents 怎么见父母

July 2026

~8 min read

For once, Jiang Qi woke before Ke Yan. He shifted his aching, languid body and reached to touch Ke Yan's face—then saw the ring on his hand. Smiling, he covered his face. So happy.

Ke Yan had proposed to him yesterday. Jiang Qi looked at the ring on his finger, the same as the one he'd bought Ke Yan — very plain, both engraved: "KY & JQ."

He ran a finger over it, then slipped it off to look at the inner band — and yes, there was an engraving inside too, but numbers. He looked closely: 2008.2015.12.25.

December 25, 2008 was the day they truly got together; December 25, 2015, the day they proposed.

Jiang Qi felt he and Ke Yan really were of one mind.

He too had had the ring engraved with both their initials, and with these dates of such meaning.

He slipped the ring back on, kissed it, kissed Ke Yan, then burrowed back into his arms to sleep.

It was Saturday, and the best thing about Saturday was sleeping until you woke on your own, so Jiang Qi had no wish to get up early.

At ten, Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao arrived downstairs at Jiang Qi and Ke Yan's. The driver opened the door; Lan Ruyin got out, Ke Shao got out on her own. Lan Ruyin checked the time — surely the two were awake by now.

They were not.

Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao were already at the door. Lan Ruyin unlocked it and found shoes scattered across the entrance.

Looking up, she found clothes strewn across the floor from the door to the living room.

Coats, a belt, trousers, sweaters — winter clothes, layers of them, especially Jiang Qi's.

Looking at the mess on the floor, Lan Ruyin frowned, and Ke Shao saw it too, exclaiming, "Looks like they had quite the passionate night — and probably aren't up yet."

"Honestly. What time is it." Lan Ruyin picked her way along the trail of clothes to the bedroom door.

Ke Shao followed.

Thinking the two might still be asleep, Ke Shao went to knock.

"Wait." Lan Ruyin stopped her and simply opened the door.

Sure enough, the two were still asleep, curtains drawn tight.

Lan Ruyin stepped softly to the bedside and looked down: Ke Yan lay higher up, an arm around someone whose only visible feature was a black-haired head. No need to look to know who.

Ke Shao watched from the other side, thinking that if Jiang Qi woke suddenly he'd get a fright.

Ke Yan woke first and found Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao watching them. Without panic or surprise, he carefully sat up and tucked the quilt around the still-sleeping Jiang Qi. Neither man was dressed.

"What are you two doing here?" Ke Yan's voice was a little hoarse.

"We came about something important. But why are you still asleep this late?" Lan Ruyin remembered Ke Yan was never one to sleep in — usually up at 7 sharp.

"What is it?" Ke Yan checked the time.

"…" Lan Ruyin fell silent — she'd just spotted the scratch marks on Ke Yan's shoulder, and how could she not know where those came from. She glanced at the still-sleeping Jiang Qi and raised her voice a little. "Young people should learn some restraint."

"Shh." Ke Yan signaled her to keep it down.

That left Lan Ruyin thoroughly exasperated, and Ke Shao couldn't help laughing out loud.

"Mm." The noise woke Jiang Qi, who mumbled drowsily, "Ke Yan?"

"Mm, awake?" Ke Yan answered, stroking the head burrowed in the quilt.

"Mm." Jiang Qi poked his head out, opened his eyes — and suddenly saw Ke Shao at the bedside, jumped, instinctively looked to Ke Yan, then saw Lan Ruyin, and was thoroughly startled, shooting upright. "Auntie? And — Sister?"

As Jiang Qi sat up, the quilt slipped, baring his shoulders and upper body.

Ke Yan whipped the quilt up at once, covering him.

Jiang Qi realized he had nothing on, his face flaming, and shrank toward Ke Yan.

Ke Shao had seen it all clearly — the bruises mottling Jiang Qi's skin, all kiss-marks — and gave a wolf-whistle like some lady hooligan.

Jiang Qi felt he hadn't a shred of face left — undressed, with Ke Yan's mother and sister right there — and buried his face in the quilt, mortified.

"You two step out first; we'll change and come down." Ke Yan sent Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao out.

Lan Ruyin said nothing, just gave Ke Yan a look and went. Ke Shao winked at Ke Yan and murmured, "Little brother, quite the heated night, hm?"

Then she followed Lan Ruyin out, pulling the door shut behind her.

"Come out now." Ke Yan pulled the quilt off Jiang Qi.

"Why is it always your mother who catches me like this?" Jiang Qi was crestfallen.

"There's nothing wrong with it. Your face is all red." Ke Yan stroked his cheek.

"Is it?"

"Mm. I'll get your clothes — sit and wait." Ke Yan got out of bed to fetch them, not bothering to throw anything on himself.

He quickly brought Jiang Qi's clothes and his own, and by the time they'd dressed and come down, Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao were already on the sofa with coffee.

Jiang Qi saw the clothes strewn on the floor and quietly gathered them up, remembering how last night, the moment he and Ke Yan got home, they hadn't been able to wait, right there at the door…

"Sit." Lan Ruyin waved them over.

"Mom, what brings you here today?" Jiang Qi and Ke Yan sat, and naturally Ke Yan spoke first.

Lan Ruyin took in Jiang Qi's pale-yellow top and white trousers — the soft yellow suited him; nothing odd about a twenty-seven-year-old wearing such a color.

She set down her cup. "It's this: since you two are together now, and your father and I have agreed to it, your sister and I came to discuss with you which day to go visit Jiang Qi's parents. The families ought to meet — and it'd be a good chance to talk."

"Meet the parents? What do you think, Jiang Qi?" Ke Yan wanted Jiang Qi's view; whatever Jiang Qi said, went.

Ke Shao looked at her brother in surprise. From how he and Jiang Qi were before, it hadn't been like this — Ke Yan usually decided things and then sought Jiang Qi's opinion; now it seemed he wanted Jiang Qi's say-so before he'd commit to anything.

"All right. I'll let my mom and dad know." Jiang Qi glanced at Ke Yan and nodded.

"Good. Call me once you've decided. I'll head off now — you two tidy this place up." Having scolded them, Lan Ruyin turned to Ke Shao. "Come to the mall with me; we'll buy a meeting gift."

Ke Shao agreed and stood, and the two were about to leave when Ke Yan, struck by a thought, called them back. "Mom, wait."

"What is it?"

"Once the day's set, don't visit Jiang Qi's home. You and Dad should come here instead."

"Why?" Lan Ruyin didn't follow.

"We're both men. If you and Dad visit Jiang Qi's home, it won't feel right." His family might feel as though they were marrying off a daughter. Meeting here avoided that imbalance.

Jiang Qi glanced at Ke Yan, understanding his meaning, his heart warm and moved — Ke Yan always thought of the things he himself hadn't.

Lan Ruyin looked at the two of them. When had her son grown so considerate? Then her gaze fixed — on their hands. "You two proposed?"

"Yes. We're married — though we haven't held the wedding." Ke Yan showed her the rings on his and Jiang Qi's hands.

"You plan to hold a wedding?" Ke Shao had seen them too, but hadn't expected Ke Yan to think of a wedding.

"Of course. Proposal, wedding — whichever it is, I'll do them all. These are all things I want to do for Jiang Qi."

Jiang Qi hadn't expected a wedding either; to him, the proposal already counted as good as married. "Ke Yan, we're practically married this way already."

"That was a proposal. I'm giving you a real wedding." Ke Yan was firm.

"I've no objection to a wedding. As long as you two are happy, I'm glad." Lan Ruyin smiled — she'd truly accepted Jiang Qi now, because today she'd seen, once again, that Ke Yan loved him more deeply than she'd imagined, and they'd proposed besides; a wedding would be good.

"Thank you, Mom." Jiang Qi suddenly thanked Lan Ruyin loudly. "I'll love Ke Yan well, I promise. Thank you for accepting me like this."

"No need for thanks. I'm off now." Her smile deepened.

"Congratulations." Ke Shao offered hers.

"Thank you, Sister." Jiang Qi said again.

Once Lan Ruyin and Ke Shao had gone, Ke Yan suddenly scooped Jiang Qi up. "Come on, wife — time to go see your mom and dad."

Jiang Qi wrapped his arms around Ke Yan's neck and said, smiling, "All right."
