The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 148

Same as Always 老样子

July 2026

~6 min read

Ke Yan helped the drunk Jiang Qi into the car. Jiang Qi leaned against the seat and shook his head, uncomfortable.

"What is it? Feeling rough?"

"Mm, the smell in the car's bothering me."

Ke Yan lowered the windows. "Drunk?"

"No." Jiang Qi shook his head. "Didn't drink that much."

"Want some water?" Ke Yan produced a bottle.

"No — let's just go home."

"All right." Ke Yan reclined the seat for him first, and Jiang Qi shut his eyes to sleep.

Ke Yan took off his coat, draped it over him, and drove on.

Once Jiang Qi was nearly asleep, he raised the windows — too cold to leave them down.

By the time they reached home Jiang Qi was sound asleep.

Ke Yan called his name a few times, but Jiang Qi only stirred. He lifted him out and carried him upstairs, laid him on the bed, then ran a bath.

When the water was ready, he undressed Jiang Qi, wrapped him in a large towel, and carried him to the bathroom.

The temperature just right, Jiang Qi soaked comfortably, opened his eyes, and looked at Ke Yan. "Ke Yan — because of you I've gotten so lazy."

"Then be lazier." Not a word of complaint, not a trace of impatience; Ke Yan gently washed him.

Jiang Qi draped lazily over the tub's edge, the warm water making him drowsier.

Ke Yan didn't wash him long; nearly done, he wrapped Jiang Qi in a soft fluffy towel and carried him to the bed.

He tucked him in, took a quick shower, then gathered Jiang Qi into his arms and fell asleep at once.

The next day, oddly, Jiang Qi woke before Ke Yan, his body bare under the covers but clean and refreshed — Ke Yan must have bathed him.

He shifted carefully in Ke Yan's arms.

"Awake?" Ke Yan woke instantly and kissed his forehead.

"Mm — got carried away last night, drank a bit much."

"Less next time. Oh — how's the shop hiring going?"

"One person applied. We open the day after tomorrow, though — don't know if anyone else will come these two days." Jiang Qi thought it had been a fair while, and only one applicant.

"Wait a bit — maybe someone will turn up these two days."

"Mm."

No sooner said than Jiang Qi's phone rang.

He looked at the nightstand — not there. "Where's my phone?"

Ke Yan stopped him from getting up undressed and catching cold, hooked the trousers Jiang Qi had worn yesterday up off the floor, and fished the phone from the pocket.

He'd dropped the trousers on the floor last night and forgotten the phone in them.

By the time Jiang Qi took it, the call had ended.

He rang back, and a young man's voice answered. "Hello — are you the bookshop owner? I'm here about the job."

"Yes. Are you at the shop door now?"

"I am. Where are you?"

"At home." Jiang Qi glanced at Ke Yan.

"Ah? Could you come over now?"

"Not right away — could you come this afternoon instead?" He didn't want to rush over; he hadn't even had breakfast.

"Then I'll wait at the café next door — good time for breakfast anyway."

The man clearly didn't want a wasted trip. "All right then—though I may be a while."

He hung up and looked at Ke Yan. "Someone's come to apply."

"Then let's get up — I'll make breakfast." Ke Yan checked the time; about time to rise.

"No — I'll make it today. You wore yourself out bathing me last night."

"All right — let's see if you can manage a new breakfast today."

"Even if it's not new, if it looks good it'll taste good too."

Jiang Qi's breakfasts were invariably congee with side dishes or eggs, bread, and milk.

The two sat up, though only Ke Yan got out of bed.

Jiang Qi sat waiting for Ke Yan to bring his clothes.

Ke Yan soon did; dressed, Jiang Qi went to wash and headed down to cook.

Ke Yan took his time — he figured Jiang Qi would make bread today, congee taking longer. Washed up and downstairs, Jiang Qi had breakfast ready: same as always, a cup of hot milk, two slices of toast, a half-scorched egg — with fruit added today.

Seeing Ke Yan come down, Jiang Qi called him to eat — then caught Ke Yan's "I-knew-it" look.

"It's different today — I even cut fruit."

"I'm very pleased. Sit and eat." Ke Yan took his hand and sat.

"That girl who applied seemed all right. I wonder if the one applying today will have left before we get there." Jiang Qi fretted around a bite of toast.

"He won't."

"How do you know?"

"Saying he'd wait for you means he cares about the job. If you're worried, call and say you're on your way."

"No need. If he cares, he'll wait; if not, my calling to keep him there means he doesn't really want the job anyway."

With that settled, Jiang Qi stopped worrying that the man might tire of waiting and leave.

When the two arrived they didn't see anyone at the door, but Jiang Qi remembered the man said the café, so he checked there.

Being the weekend, it wasn't crowded, and Jiang Qi spotted at a glance a young man by the window — wasn't sure if it was him.

The young man by the window suddenly turned, saw Jiang Qi, stood, and came straight over.

"Are you the bookshop owner?"

"Yes — you're here about the job?"

"I am — thought you'd come much later, but you're quite early." He checked the time.

"Come on over."

Jiang Qi took him to the shop; Ke Yan had it open.

"What's your name?" Jiang Qi asked.

"Zhu Zihao."

"I'm Jiang Qi. It's like this — one-month trial, then permanent if you do well."

"Understood. No issue with the pay either — when do I start?"

"Next Monday."

"No problem. I'll be off then — I've things to do." Zhu Zihao stood.

"All right — sorry for making you wait so long this morning."

"It's fine." And Zhu Zihao left.

"Why not be a bit fiercer — you don't seem like a boss." Ke Yan had been in a corner watching Jiang Qi interview; once the man left, he came out and pinched Jiang Qi's cheek.

"Who says a boss has to be fierce?" Jiang Qi swatted his hand away.

"I want to take you to try on suits today." Unbothered at being swatted, Ke Yan remembered it was about time to fit the suits — for the wedding.

"Try on suits? Don't you have plenty already?"

"Wedding suits."

"What? Oh — all right then." At the word wedding, Jiang Qi felt a flutter of excitement and nerves. Right — it was about time to start preparing.
