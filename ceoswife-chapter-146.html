Chapter 146

Happy Just to Have You Beside Me 有你陪就很幸福

July 2026

~5 min read

When Yang Shaoyu got home Lin Zirui was still asleep; knowing he'd finished at noon, Yang Shaoyu figured he'd slept the afternoon away.

"Zirui, Zirui." He woke him — sleep any longer and Lin Zirui wouldn't sleep tonight.

"Mm — you're back?" Lin Zirui opened his eyes, saw it was Yang Shaoyu, and stretched.

"Mm, get up — sleep more and you'll be up all night."

Lin Zirui sat up and said, "Eldest Brother and Second Brother came to the hospital today."

"What for?" That Lin Zirui still called them that meant he didn't blame them.

"The family had them bring my things out to me." Lin Zirui was expressionless, as if it didn't touch him.

Yang Shaoyu held him, heart aching.

"The boxes are still in the car — you'll have to help me carry them up later."

"All right. But eat first — what do you feel like?"

"Something spicy."

"Done — I'll cook now. Get up, put on a sweater, don't pad around barefoot." Yang Shaoyu nagged like a mother hen.

Lin Zirui burrowed back into the covers and laughed to himself. "I know."

Yang Shaoyu went to cook. Lin Zirui wanted spicy food, but Yang Shaoyu kept the heat moderate; too much at night would be hard on his stomach.

By the time Lin Zirui came out of his shower, the food was nearly ready.

"You're so domestic." Cooking and housework both.

"Come eat." Yang Shaoyu pretended not to hear the "domestic" and dished up two bowls of rice.

Lin Zirui sat, saw the dishes he liked, and dug in. So hungry — he'd skipped lunch.

"Don't choke."

"I know. Oh — next chance we get, let's have Eldest and Second Brother over for a meal."

"All right, I'll cook then." Yang Shaoyu was glad Lin Zirui could loosen the knot in his heart; he didn't want Zirui bottling up the hurt alone.

"Zirui, does it hurt — in here?" Afraid the question would sadden him, but more afraid he'd keep it bottled, Yang Shaoyu asked.

"Hm?" Lin Zirui looked up around a mouthful of meat.

"Your parents treating you that way — does it hurt?"

He set down his chopsticks. "Not much anymore. Once, it would have. I'd have thought: my parents drove me out, and now I'm alone—working alone, eating alone, coming home to an empty house. That was what I feared most. But now I have you. You're better to me than my parents were; you love me, and I love you. With you beside me, I'm happy. I'm not afraid anymore."

"Not just me — Mom and Dad too. They'll cherish you."

"Yes — so I'm not sad." Lin Zirui smiled.

"As long as you're not sad, I'm at ease. Eat up — you skipped lunch again, didn't you?"

"How do you know?"

"You're eating this fast — you've clearly been hungry a long time."

"True — too sleepy, in a rush to get home and sleep." Lin Zirui didn't dare meet his eyes.

"Do this again and I'll be cross."

"I know — eat up." He coaxingly put a piece of meat in Yang Shaoyu's bowl.

Yang Shaoyu ate it, and Lin Zirui smiled. He was unusually agreeable today. Usually, if Zirui skipped a meal, Shaoyu would scold him and come to the hospital the next day to make sure he ate lunch.

Sure enough, though Yang Shaoyu hadn't been angry the night before, he appeared at the hospital at noon the next day to supervise his meal.

"What do you want today? I'll go buy it." Yang Shaoyu came to Lin Zirui's office, and there he was, work-time though it was, with no sign of going to eat.

"Why are you here?"

"Because I knew you wouldn't eat on time." He flicked Lin Zirui's head.

Lin Zirui blinked his dry, aching eyes. "Lost track of time."

"What's wrong — eyes hurting?" Yang Shaoyu noticed the unnatural blinking.

"Mm, been reading too long."

Seeing the stack of books on the desk, Yang Shaoyu had him close his eyes and did eye-relaxation exercises for him.

"You know how to do this?" Lin Zirui closed his eyes, leaning back, comfortable.

"You're a bit nearsighted, and I've seen you rub your eyes, so I learned it specially — not sure if it helps."

"It helps — it feels good."

"Good. What do you want for lunch?"

"Ramen, with egg and chili."

"All right, I'll go get it — it's very cold out today."

"All right — that's enough; I'll wait for you to come back." Lin Zirui felt much better.

Yang Shaoyu went for the noodles. Lin Zirui rested at his desk a while, checked the time — Shaoyu should be nearly back.

He left the office and walked slowly to the nurses' station.

Since the elevator opened right onto the station, he sat there to wait for Yang Shaoyu.

"Doctor Lin, waiting for someone?" a nurse asked.

"Mm."

"For Mr. Yang?" The nurses knew Mr. Yang was close with Doctor Lin, often coming to see him.

"Yes — he went to buy noodles."

"Oh, Mr. Yang's so good to you, even buying you noodles."

"He is." As she spoke, Yang Shaoyu came up in the elevator, two bowls of noodles in hand.

"Waiting here for me?" He saw Lin Zirui come out of the station.

"Mm, I'm starving." Lin Zirui caught the smell, and his stomach growled.

"Mr. Yang." The nurses greeted him.

"Hello." He knew several of them by now.

"Here to eat with Doctor Lin?" Handsome and never impatient when they chatted with him, Yang Shaoyu was popular with the nurses—especially the unmarried ones.

"Yes — Zirui always forgets to eat. Nothing for it; I have to come eat with him myself." Grumbling words, but a tender look on his face.

"Let's go, I'm hungry." Hearing Yang Shaoyu say that in front of so many people made Lin Zirui shy.

"All right, let's go." The instant Lin Zirui said he was hungry, Yang Shaoyu dropped everything and hurried him back to the office to eat.

In truth, Yang Shaoyu wasn't patient with everyone. He was kinder to them because they were Lin Zirui's colleagues—and that, too, was a kind of tenderness.
