Chapter 97

What Are You Up To? 想干嘛

July 2026

~6 min read

Lin Zirui came out of the bathroom to find Yang Shaoyu blocking the doorway. "What are you doing?"

"Let's go get breakfast."

"No need. I have to go home and change anyway."

"Wear mine." Yang Shaoyu produced a set of clothes.

This time Lin Zirui didn't refuse — the shirt he had on was creased now, and it smelled.

He changed. A little big. He stepped out of the washroom.

"Let's go eat, then." Yang Shaoyu had already changed too.

"Mm—" Lin Zirui was about to agree when his phone rang. He saw it was the hospital and silently thanked his luck that it still had charge and hadn't switched off. "Hello — yes. Yes. I'll be right there." He hung up and looked at Yang Shaoyu. "I have to get back to the hospital."

"All right, I'll drive you." Yang Shaoyu grabbed the car keys and they headed out.

"Quick as you can, please." A call after hours usually meant something urgent — or that they were short on doctors.

"Right. Belt on."

"Mm." Lin Zirui did as told. He glanced over at Yang Shaoyu driving. He couldn't have breakfast with him after all — and Yang Shaoyu hadn't voiced a word of complaint, or impatience.

Lin Zirui smiled a little. "I'll treat you next time."

"Hm?" Yang Shaoyu thought he'd misheard.

"I'll treat you to a meal next time."

"All right." Confirming he'd heard right, Yang Shaoyu broke into a wide, delighted grin.

Watching him grin like a fool, Lin Zirui couldn't help smiling too.

The car reached the hospital. The moment it stopped, Lin Zirui unbuckled and ran inside.

Yang Shaoyu parked and followed.

Parking was hard to find, so by the time he got in, Lin Zirui was already prepping for the operating room, standing among the other doctors — the youngest of them by far.

Lin Zirui didn't see Yang Shaoyu slip in with the group of doctors.

Yang Shaoyu sat, as he had last time, on the chairs outside, waiting for Lin Zirui to come out, ignoring the looks the patients' families gave him.

Which was fair enough — every seat outside an operating room was taken by family of the patients, and here was a man with no connection to any of them. How could they not find it odd?

Meanwhile, Jiang Qi and Ke Yan had finished breakfast and sat in the living room a while before Ke Yan took Jiang Qi on a tour of the house.

Ke Yan led him out the back door, to another garden — though not the same one as last night.

"So there's a garden back here too." Jiang Qi thought this one prettier than the front.

"Mm. The front one's for guests. This one's for family." Ke Yan walked on, holding his hand.

"Oh — Ke Yan, there's a stream!" Jiang Qi spotted the brook ahead, delighted, and not a small one either.

"How is there a stream?" He plunged a hand in to play with the water.

"It comes down off the hill behind." The Ke house backed onto the hillside, which was why it ran.

Jiang Qi pulled off his shoes, sat down at the bank, and dangled his feet in.

The back garden wasn't as large as the front, but it was far cozier — a lounge chair, a swing, a little pavilion, a small bridge, the brook, and any number of flowers Jiang Qi couldn't name.

"Ke Yan, do you think my mom and dad will accept you?" The question came to Jiang Qi out of nowhere. If Ke Yan's mother had found it so hard to accept him, then what about his own?

"They will. Because I'll work at it." Work at becoming family with yours.

"All right. I'll work at it too — maybe Auntie will end up liking me a lot someday."

Ke Yan laughed at that, and copied him, putting his own feet into the water.

"Hm?" Jiang Qi felt something tickle the soles of his feet. He looked down — fish, of all things.

"Just little ones." Ke Yan saw them too.

"This is nice."

Jiang Qi sat there at his ease, hand in Ke Yan's — and, thinking of having to work tomorrow, was suddenly seized by a reluctance to go.

"I've gone lazy. Don't even want to go to work." Jiang Qi murmured.

"What do you want to do when we're old?" Ke Yan asked, out of the blue.

"Old? Mm — open a bookshop. Read at my leisure, sell books, tidy the shelves, eat a slice of cake now and then."

"And me?" Ke Yan had listened to the whole thing and not heard himself in it.

"You're there too, of course. Without you I don't want any of it." Jiang Qi said it, and colored a little.

Ke Yan bent and kissed him; Jiang Qi kissed back, both arms looping around Ke Yan's neck.

Ke Yan caught at his lips and tongue — and then Jiang Qi shoved him off, hard.

"What is it?" Ke Yan looked at him, lips kiss-reddened.

Jiang Qi glanced around, and only let out a breath once he'd confirmed there was no one. "I forgot we're at your house. It'd be bad if someone saw."

Watching Jiang Qi go all furtive, Ke Yan's face darkened again. Better to head home early today.

As it turned out, he didn't even get to head home early in the afternoon — didn't get lunch — because a snag had come up with an overseas partnership, and Ke Yan had to go in to the company.

"You go on to the office. I'll cab it home." Jiang Qi looked at Ke Yan, keys in hand.

"I'll drop you first."

"No — go to the office first." Jiang Qi was firm.

"That's right. Don't worry, I'll have the driver take Jiang Qi back in a bit," Lan Ruyin put in.

"All right then, I'm off." Ke Yan nodded and left, ringing Yang Shaoyu on his way to tell him there was work.

Ke Shao and her family were still asleep, so the living room now held no one but the servants, Lan Ruyin, and Jiang Qi. Jiang Qi smiled stiffly. "There's no need for the driver — I'll just take a cab home myself."

"Don't go back so early. Stay another night, and go straight to the office in the morning."

"Ah? That wouldn't be right." Without Ke Yan at his side, Jiang Qi was a bundle of nerves in this house, thank you very much.

"What — you don't like it here?" Lan Ruyin's tone cooled.

"No, no — I love it here." Jiang Qi shook his head quickly.

"Good. Go watch some television for now. You can come shopping with me this afternoon." And with that she walked off, giving Jiang Qi no chance to get a word in.

His stomach gave an uneasy turn. Why had Ke Yan's mother suddenly told him to stay? And to go shopping with her?

Unable to make sense of it, he finally gave up and followed Auntie Ke's advice to watch television. He still wondered whether the problem would send Ke Yan on a business trip. If it was serious, that seemed likely.

He said he'd stopped wondering, but with the television on he took none of it in. What on earth was Ke Yan's mother up to?
