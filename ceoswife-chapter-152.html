Thoughtful 贴心

With work and school the next day, Yang Shaoyu, Lin Zirui, the eldest brother, and Xiao De all headed home before evening.

With the children all gone at once, the house fell quiet. Watching Father and Mother Yang see them off at the door, Lin Zirui said to Yang Shaoyu, "Let's come back often. I like being with Mom and Dad — and they live here just the two of them, it's lonely."

"All right, we'll come often — though my dad's always off fishing with friends, and my mom's always out for afternoon tea, so no need to worry about them being lonely." Yang Shaoyu lifted Lin Zirui's hand to his lips and kissed it.

"Mm."

It was evening by the time they got home; luckily Mother Yang had sent them off with side dishes, so they only had to cook some rice and reheat them.

As Yang Shaoyu took the dishes from the trunk, Lin Zirui spotted two suspiciously familiar figures behind another car. They looked remarkably like his eldest and second brothers. He blinked, and they were gone. Had he imagined them?

"What is it?" Yang Shaoyu saw him drift toward the car.

"Nothing — I think I saw wrong." He looked, but his brothers were nowhere; he must have imagined it.

"Let's go up, then."

"All right."

Once the two had boarded the elevator, the hidden Lin brothers came out.

"Phew — Xiao Rui nearly saw us." The second brother let out a breath.

"Come on, let's go." They had only come to check on Lin Zirui. Now that they had seen him, they had better leave before Xiao Rui caught them.

Home, while Yang Shaoyu cooked and reheated, Lin Zirui thought a moment, took out his phone, and called the eldest brother.

It picked up fast. "Hello — Xiao Rui, what is it?"

"Nothing — just wondering what you and Second Brother are up to." He really thought he'd seen them.

The eldest brother startled — had Xiao Rui spotted them? He glanced at the second brother and answered calmly, "Your second brother and I are on our way home from the office."

"The office? Isn't it the weekend?"

"Oh — something came up at work." In his fluster he'd forgotten it was the weekend.

"Oh — right, this week come have a meal together, you and Second Brother. We said we would and haven't yet." Lin Zirui believed him.

"Sure — I'll hang up now." The eldest brother rang off and exchanged a look with the second. "Xiao Rui says to have a meal together this week."

"Really? I was afraid he'd forget." The second brother had been waiting on that meal, fretting that Lin Zirui never brought it up.

At home, they found their parents and little sister already eating. Watching the three of them together, the eldest brother imagined Xiao Rui returning to no warmth from his parents while they doted on Lin Le. How that must have hurt. Yet he had no right to judge; hadn't he favored their sister too?

The second brother, blunt by nature and thinking much the same, spoke up at once. "Dad, Mom — you drove Zirui out of the house; don't you worry about him at all?"

"What's there to worry about? Your father and I gave him a chance. He insisted on being with that man—for the Lin family's reputation, we had no choice but to drive him out." Just thinking of Lin Zirui made Mother Lin angry.

"Yes — if he breaks up with that man, I'll let him come home."

"I really don't know how you two are parents." The second brother dropped the line and went straight to his room.

The eldest brother lost his appetite too, and glanced at his parents. "Pay Xiao Rui a little attention sometimes. Cherish him a bit. Don't disappoint him again."

Father and Mother Lin were awkward, dressed down by their eldest son for the first time.

Lin Le promptly said, "Lin Zirui's the one in the wrong — why aren't you scolding him?"

The eldest brother had no wish to deal with Lin Le; it was the whole family spoiling her that had made her, he now saw, utterly without manners.

Lin Le huffed and stopped speaking to him.

For once, Father and Mother Lin didn't demand that their eldest son apologize to his sister. After what he had said about Lin Zirui, taking Lin Le's side would only make things more awkward.

Monday. Jiang Qi went out with Ke Yan as usual, but got out not at Ke Group — at the bookshop across from it.

It wasn't work time yet, so when Jiang Qi arrived not a single employee was there.

He unlocked the door, went in and switched on the lights, while Ke Yan turned on the heating.

"Ke Yan, this feels wonderful." He was a bookshop owner now.

"As long as you like it. Come here." Ke Yan was boiling water, and called him over.

"I'm boiling water now. If it runs out, have the staff make more. If you get hungry, buy some cake at the café next door. Come find me at the company for lunch." Ke Yan went on and on.

Jiang Qi hugged him from behind around the waist. "I know — and I'm just across the way, stop worrying; I'll come for lunch."

"Good — but I forgot one thing."

"What?"

"Forgot to buy you a hand-warmer — your hands are like ice." Ke Yan had touched Jiang Qi's cold hands.

"It's fine, I'll buy one tomorrow." Jiang Qi didn't mind.

Just then the door opened; Jiang Qi quickly let go of Ke Yan's waist, and saw Wang Xiaoyu come in.

She'd assumed only the boss would be in this early, but seeing Ke Yan she paused — not knowing who he was, though he seemed somehow familiar.

"You're here — quite early." Jiang Qi greeted her.

"Mm — first day, so I came early." Still a dozen minutes to work time.

"This is Wang Xiaoyu, my first employee," Jiang Qi told Ke Yan.

"Hello." Ke Yan said, coolly.

"Hello." Facing Ke Yan made Wang Xiaoyu a little nervous; that cold face gave her a touch of fear.

"I'll step out a moment." Ke Yan wanted to buy Jiang Qi something.

"All right — go straight to the company after, no need to come back." Jiang Qi told him.

Ke Yan left. Jiang Qi and Wang Xiaoyu began tidying the shop and dusting the tables; soon Zhu Zihao arrived as well.

Jiang Qi settled into the soft sofa with a book while Wang Xiaoyu and Zhu Zihao arranged the shelves. They kept glancing at the door for customers—and at their boss, who didn't seem the least worried by the lack of them.

Not long into his reading, Ke Yan came back. Jiang Qi stood. "Not at the company yet?"

"Mm — I bought you a few things first, then I'll go." Ke Yan handed him a bag.

Jiang Qi looked: a hand-warmer, milk tea, and the bread he liked.

He couldn't help smiling. Ke Yan was still so thoughtful.

"Off to the company, then."

"Mm — I'll come find you at noon." Jiang Qi saw Ke Yan to the door, watching until he'd crossed the road and gone into the company before shutting it and going back in.

Now Wang Xiaoyu finally understood why Ke Yan looked familiar — he was the very image of one of the chibi figures painted on the wall.
