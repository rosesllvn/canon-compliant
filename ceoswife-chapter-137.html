Chapter 137

Going Somewhere 去一个地方

July 2026

~5 min read

Jiang Qi and Ke Yan got back to the Jiang house right at lunchtime, so they ate first. For once everyone was home today, even Jiang Bu's son, Jiang Wen.

Jiang Wen was three, and a hearty eater — small but a little chubby for it.

Seeing Jiang Qi arrive, he ran over wanting to be held.

Jiang Qi bent and scooped him up.

Jiang Wen kissed his cheek and said, excited, "Little Uncle, Xiao Wen hasn't seen you in so long."

Jiang Qi kissed his plump little face too. "Uncle hasn't seen Xiao Wen in ages either. Have you been good lately?"

"Mm, good." Xiao Wen nodded, then noticed Ke Yan behind Jiang Qi. "Uncle, who's he?"

"He's an uncle too." Jiang Qi set Xiao Wen down beside Ke Yan.

"Oh. Is he Little Uncle's boyfriend?"

Jiang Qi was startled. "How do you know that?"

Xiao Wen pointed at Jiang Lü. "Auntie said so."

Everyone looked at Jiang Lü.

She shrank into her collar and gave a couple of dry laughs.

"By the way, Mom — set a date, and meet Ke Yan's parents," Jiang Qi said, since the conversation had landed there anyway.

"Meet them?" Mother Jiang hadn't expected it so soon.

"Yes. My parents would like to meet you." Ke Yan set down his chopsticks.

"All right — but where?"

"I was thinking at Jiang Qi's and my home — the place you came to last time."

She had visited once and liked the suggestion. She was also relieved he had not proposed the Jiang house, which might feel like his family asking for a bride's hand. She nodded. "When shall we meet Ke Yan's parents?"

He considered. "Next Saturday, then — you're both off, and your mother and I can get ready these few days."

"All right." Jiang Qi took a sip of water; the food was a little salty today.

"Hm? Brother, is that a — ring on your hand?" Jiang Lü had been watching her brother and brother-husband, and as Jiang Qi lifted his cup with his left hand, she spotted the ring on his finger.

"It is." Jiang Qi looked at it too.

"I can't believe you two already proposed." Yun Hui chewed her chopsticks, wishing she could have seen it.

Jiang Lü's eyes positively shone.

Mother Jiang saw the ring too. Ah — so her second son was getting married as well. Ke Yan was a man, true, but at least his circumstances weren't bad.

After lunch the two stayed on at the Jiang house, and called Lan Ruyin to let her know they'd meet Jiang Qi's parents next Saturday.

Lin Zirui had been staying at Yang Shaoyu's lately, scarcely going home at all.

Off today, he thought he'd swing by home and grab some medical books while he was at it.

He opened the door to a strangely quiet house. Inside, not a soul in the living room — but Auntie Zhao, the cook, heard him and came out of the kitchen. "Third Young Master."

"Where is everyone?"

"You don't know?" Auntie Zhao looked at him, uneasy.

"What is it?" Her pitying gaze made him uncomfortable.

"The master, the mistress, the two young masters and the young lady have gone on a three-day, two-night trip. They'll be back tomorrow evening. The servants all went along to look after them."

"But why didn't anyone tell me?" Even as he said it he felt he'd asked a foolish question, and laughed at himself, going upstairs.

Auntie Zhao watched his back as he climbed, and sighed. All of them his own blood, yet treated so differently. She shook her head; the Third Young Master would surely be heartbroken.

Once, Lin Zirui would have pretended indifference while aching inside. Now he had Shaoyu. The neglect still stung, but it could no longer break him.

He opened the wardrobe, packed clothes into a suitcase, took his important medical books, glanced at the room he'd lived in for twenty-odd years, and turned and left.

He had a home now — a home for him and Shaoyu, the two of them.

Wheeling the suitcase back, Yang Shaoyu came out the moment he heard him. "Why are you back so early?"

"No one was home. I packed a few things and came back."

"You don't seem happy."

"I'm not — though not terribly. It just doesn't feel good, being overlooked by family. A little down, that's all."

Yang Shaoyu asked no more, just lifted Lin Zirui and sat with him on the sofa. "It's all right. My parents are your parents — they're wonderful, and they'll love you."

"Really?" Lin Zirui rested his head against him.

"Let me take you somewhere." Yang Shaoyu suddenly proposed it, pulling Lin Zirui up and wrapping his scarf on for him.

"Where?" Lin Zirui didn't follow.

"Somewhere that'll make you happy." He opened the door and led him out.

"Somewhere that'll make me happy?" Lin Zirui still had no idea where.

"You'll see when we get there." Yang Shaoyu kept it a mystery.

He'd said soon, but it was a long drive before they arrived.

He pulled into a villa estate. Lin Zirui watched out the window — he'd never been here. What were they doing?

"Come on, out." Yang Shaoyu stopped the car.

"Where is this?" Yang Shaoyu led him by the hand toward the house.

"You'll know once you meet someone."

He pressed the bell, and after a moment a very lovely woman opened the door; she paused at the sight of Yang Shaoyu and Lin Zirui.

Then she smiled at Lin Zirui. "You're here — come in."

Lin Zirui hadn't the faintest idea who she was, though she seemed to know him. "And you are?"

"I'm Shaoyu's mother."

What? Lin Zirui was floored.
