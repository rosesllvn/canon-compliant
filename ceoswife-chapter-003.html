The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 3

Jealousy 吃醋

July 2026

~2 min read

Ke Yan stopped the car a short way from the office and let Jiang Qi out. "Be careful."

"Mm, I know. It's not far from here." Jiang Qi smiled and waved him off; Ke Yan nodded and drove away.

He'd arrived later than usual, and the moment he stepped inside he found the area before the elevators packed shoulder to shoulder — a jostling, clamorous crush. The sight of it gave him a headache, and he decided he'd be better off on the stairs.

Just then Xiao An, fighting her way into an elevator with all her might, spotted him, squeezed back out through the crowd, and planted herself in front of him. "Manager Jiang, how come you're so late? Usually you're already at your desk by the time I get in."

"Overslept," he answered while stealing a glance toward the elevators.

For all her breeziness, Xiao An could be remarkably observant. Catching the direction of his glance, she thumped her chest. "No worries — I'll get you in there." The words were barely out before his face went flat. He looked down at her — petite enough that she only came up to his shoulder — and said, helpless, "Xiao An. I'm rather bigger than you."

"Aiya, who's counting, why be so fussy. Heh heh." She scratched her head, laughed, then latched onto his arm and went charging forward.

Jiang Qi let her tow him along — and then felt a familiar gaze settle on him. He turned: Ke Yan, eyes narrowed to a fine line. He knew that look. It meant Ke Yan was about to be angry. He pried Xiao An loose in a hurry.

"What's wrong?" she asked, blinking at the hand he'd just freed.

"Ah — nothing. I'll walk on my own."

By rights Ke Yan should have reached the office well ahead of Jiang Qi, but he'd run into Director Huang of the Boya Group on the way and stopped to trade a few words, which cost him time. He walked in to the usual murmur of good-mornings — and caught sight of Xiao An clinging to Jiang Qi's arm. He knew perfectly well she had no interest in Jiang Qi. The sight closed his chest up tight all the same.

Trailing a step behind, his assistant saw his boss stop short mid-stride, staring at something ahead, and followed the look.

The assistant's name was Yang Shaoyu — an old friend of Ke Yan's, pulled into the company to help out the moment he graduated, and the only person there who knew the two were a couple. One glance told him his friend was eight-tenths of the way to losing his temper. And when Ke Yan lost his temper, he liked to dump all his work on Yang Shaoyu, who would end up half-dead, worked ragged by day and kept late into the night. So he hurried over to smooth things down.
