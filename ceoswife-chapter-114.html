Silly Wife 傻媳妇

July 2026

~5 min read

The next morning, the moment Yang Shaoyu woke he felt Lin Zirui's forehead — no fever, thankfully.

Looking at Lin Zirui sleeping soundly in his arms, Yang Shaoyu grinned foolishly, and grinned wider still remembering last night.

Lin Zirui slept a while longer, then woke to find Yang Shaoyu beaming at him like an idiot. He smiled back, then said, "Shaoyu, I'm starving."

"What? Wait here, I'll make breakfast." Yang Shaoyu rose at once, but moved carefully to keep cold air from slipping beneath the covers.

Watching Yang Shaoyu's back as he dressed, Lin Zirui smiled. Truly happy — to have someone who could care for him like this, love him like this.

The day came for Jiang Qi to visit the Ke house. He was nervous, but less than before. If Ke Xin refused, they would keep trying; with Ke Yan beside him, it would be all right.

The two slept until they woke naturally, then Ke Yan got the bread and Jiang Qi the milk. Jiang Qi ate a couple of bites and stopped.

"That's all you're eating?" Ke Yan refilled the empty glass with half a cup of warm milk.

"No appetite this morning." Jiang Qi drank the half cup down.

"Eat a little more, then." Two bites wasn't enough.

"Mm." Obediently Jiang Qi ate another slice of bread.

After breakfast, they put on their coats and left—only to hurry back for the two gift bags waiting on the living-room table.

As they came down, Ke Yan's car was just being brought round; Jiang Qi got in, and they drove to the Ke house together.

When they arrived, only Lan Ruyin was there, sitting on the sofa with a magazine; where Ke Xin was, no one knew.

"Hello, Auntie." Jiang Qi handed her the gifts.

"What's this?"

"Black tea."

"And the other bag?" She'd noticed the second one in his hand.

"Books — for Uncle."

Lan Ruyin smiled. "How did you know he likes to read?"

"Ke Yan told me."

She'd assumed Jiang Qi would claim he'd observed it himself; she hadn't expected such candor.

"Where's Dad?" Ke Yan asked.

"He's unwell."

"Is it bad?" Jiang Qi's heart lurched. Had he and Ke Yan's business made Ke Xin ill with anger?

"How is he now?" Ke Yan was concerned.

"It's nothing — he caught a chill, a bit of a fever."

"Let's go see him." Ke Yan rose, and Jiang Qi rose too, bowing slightly to Lan Ruyin before following Ke Yan to Ke Xin's room.

Ke Xin, miserable with fever, was sleeping — but woke the moment Ke Yan and Jiang Qi drew near. Seeing Jiang Qi, his expression didn't change at all; he opened his mouth, and Jiang Qi braced for whatever he might say.

"Hungry." Ke Xin produced just that one word.

Jiang Qi blinked. "I'll go ask Auntie Li to make something," Ke Yan said, and went out.

Alone with Ke Xin in the room, Jiang Qi bent and said, "Uncle."

"Mm." Cool.

"I brought you books." Jiang Qi took them out and offered them.

"Books?" Ke Xin was surprised Jiang Qi had brought him books.

"Mm. Ke Yan said you like to read. I had someone track these down."

Ke Xin took them, and watched the way Jiang Qi's expression changed the instant Ke Yan came up — something sweet in it, a look that made you feel he was very happy.

"Have the two of you never quarreled?"

"Quarreled?" Jiang Qi blinked, seeming to think back. "Of course we have — together this many years, we've had a few rows."

"How did you meet?" Ke Xin couldn't accept Jiang Qi yet, but he knew his son's character — once Ke Yan set his mind on something, he never changed it, and once he settled on a person, he never gave up.

"Mm…" Jiang Qi thought. "My pendant fell off the dormitory building, and Ke Yan happened to catch it. Quite a coincidence, isn't it?" Perhaps because the illness had left Ke Xin low on energy and so less unapproachable, Jiang Qi's nerve grew a little.

"And then…" Ke Xin was about to ask more when Ke Yan came in.

"Auntie Li's off today. Mom says she'll make lunch."

"Then I'll go help Auntie." Jiang Qi made to go.

"All right, go on." Ke Yan didn't stop him — Jiang Qi's cooking was dreadful, but he trusted his mother wouldn't let Jiang Qi near a wok.

Jiang Qi went down to the kitchen, and the father and son in the room sat quiet a moment before Ke Yan spoke. "Dad, well? Hard to actually dislike Jiang Qi, isn't it."

"A bit of a fool." Ke Xin found Jiang Qi rather muddleheaded.

"Foolish in a lovable way." To Ke Yan, Jiang Qi was good no matter what.

"How did the two of you end up together?"

"The first time we met, I caught the pendant he dropped. He seemed dazed and a little slow. After that, I ran into him everywhere, even at the library. Jiang Qi loves to read too. That's how it began; each meeting deepened my impression of him."

"I really wouldn't have guessed." Looking at Jiang Qi's somewhat foolish manner, Ke Xin hadn't taken him for someone fond of books.

Watching his father, Ke Yan judged he wouldn't be so opposed to Jiang Qi after all — it seemed his silly wife could breathe a little easier. The simple really were blessed; Jiang Qi was just plain likable.

Downstairs, Jiang Qi joined Lan Ruyin in the kitchen. She had grown rusty after so long away from the stove. Another servant usually covered Auntie Li's days off, but illness made Ke Xin so finicky that he would eat only a little of his wife's cooking.

Seeing Jiang Qi come to help, her face fell at once; she hadn't forgotten what his cooking was like — frankly inedible, and unsightly besides.

"Jiang Qi, you just wash and chop the vegetables. Whatever you do, don't touch the wok."

"All right." Jiang Qi didn't dare touch the wok either — Ke Xin was ill; what if eating his cooking made it worse?

So he obediently washed and chopped — though, really, was it that bad?
