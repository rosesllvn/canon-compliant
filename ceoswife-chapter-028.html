The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 28

Stammering, Truly Anxious 支支吾吾真着急

July 2026

~5 min read

The next day Jiang Qi meant to tell Ke Yan he'd be at Liu Hao's birthday party and couldn't read with him — but he searched all morning and couldn't find him anywhere.

At noon he came back to the dorm and took out paper and pen. Should I leave him a note? After some thought he took out an unopened bottle of water and a roll of tape, and wrote: I have a birthday party today and can't keep you company reading.

Looking at the note, he slumped weakly over the desk. How do I not even have his phone number? I never asked the things I should have. Next time we meet I absolutely must remember. Resolved, he got up and taped the note to the bottle.

Bottle in hand, he jogged all the way to the library and set it down at Ke Yan's usual spot. Looking at the seat, he reached out and touched the chairback with a fingertip — then, helpless to stop himself, stroked it, and as though shocked snatched his finger back, his face a little red. He glanced around. No one saw, surely.

Toward evening, the moment class let out, Jiang Qi was hauled off by Liu Hao and Meng Qing, who collected Ye Xingwei along the way. As they walked, Jiang Qi looked at the thoroughly delighted Liu Hao. "We'll be back before the dorm locks up, right?"

Liu Hao wore a you're-such-a-bore look. "Tonight I am absolutely pulling an all-nighter."

Jiang Qi shut his mouth, deciding to slip away quietly once he'd eaten and drunk his fill.

The party was at a large restaurant not far from school — good food, fair prices, close by, so a lot of students ate there. When the four arrived, a middle-aged man in a suit stood in the lobby and greeted them with a smile. "Hello, I'm the manager. Due to an unexpected matter we've moved your private room — but not to worry, I've arranged a larger and finer one, and your friends are already waiting. Oh — and the price will be halved."

Liu Hao figured he was coming out ahead, and agreed.

The manager led them upstairs to a set of luxurious double doors and opened them — and Liu Hao, in the lead, was met with a blaze of gold: gold wallpaper, gold carpet, a gold crystal chandelier, thoroughly resplendent, dazzling enough to blind them all.

"Hey, you've arrived." Yang Shaoyu greeted them from where he leaned.

"Yang Shaoyu? What's going on here?" Liu Hao asked, taken aback.

"Oh — the manager said a schoolmate of mine happened to have his birthday the same day, but there wasn't a free room, so he offered free desserts if we'd celebrate together. I agreed. You?"

"Fine by me." Though they hardly knew each other, Liu Hao agreed readily.

The group moved inside, Ye Xingwei trailing last — all he wanted was to eat quietly and go home. But low as he tried to keep his profile, he was spotted by the cluster of girls who'd dressed with particular elegance today, and one after another they came over with wine glasses to greet him.

Everyone sat. Liu Hao and Yang Shaoyu, the two birthday boys, took one table; Ye Xingwei was ringed by women, their assorted perfumes mingling until even the most expensive scent turned strangely unpleasant. Seated at the same table, Jiang Qi looked at his fed-up expression and sighed — being too popular isn't much fun either.

He picked up the drink in front of him and took a sip — and choked, tongue burning, sticking it out. How is it alcohol? So nasty.

"Here, water." Just as he was casting about for some, a glass appeared in front of him, and without a thought he took a big gulp. Only after did he realize it had come from Ye Xingwei. He blinked, plainly not having expected him to be so considerate. "Thanks." He smiled.

Ye Xingwei kept his face cold and didn't answer; he only pushed away the person beside him, picked up a glass of wine, and went to sit on the big white sofa in the corner. The girls, deciding they were making fools of themselves, stopped pestering him.

Jiang Qi sat alone and bored, eating. The room was noisy, Liu Hao's table noisiest, with nearly everyone gathered round it. He tried a piece of fish — mm, not bad — and was savoring it, absorbed, and didn't hear someone calling him.

"J-Jiang Qi." Qiao Mi'er called softly, but he didn't react. She grew anxious, bit her lower lip, and turned for help to her sisters not far off, who made frantic go-for-it gestures.

She drew a deep breath and gave his shoulder a light pat. "H-hello, Jiang Qi?"

This time he came back to himself, and turned, surprised. "Hello — and you are?"

Seeing him actually speak to her, she grew more nervous still, her voice trembling. "H-hello."

She already said that, Jiang Qi thought, chopsticks in his mouth. Seeing how nervous she was, he set them down, pulled out the chair beside him, and said considerately, "Would you like to sit down and talk?"

She breathed out, nodded, and sat. Under the table her hands clutched at her clothes; she didn't know how to begin.

Jiang Qi got her a glass of juice. "Have a sip first, then."

Her face went a little red, and she took a small sip.

"Um, that, um, I — I..." She stammered, and after a long while still hadn't gotten a single sentence out, palms sweating.

Jiang Qi was anxious too. What on earth is she trying to say? He badly wanted to hurry her, but feared pressing would only make it harder. She seems so nervous — about what? His mind ran in scattered directions. Qiao Mi'er sat head down, clutching her clothes, unable to squeeze out a word.

Ye Xingwei, on the sofa, happened to see the scene — and watching Qiao Mi'er's bashful expression, he couldn't help knitting his brows hard, and in irritation tipped back the glass in his hand all at once.

The boy on another sofa beside him plainly saw it too — though what caught his attention more was Ye Xingwei's reaction.
