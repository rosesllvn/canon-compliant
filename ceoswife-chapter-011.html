Auntie Li 李婶

~4 min read

"Mm..." Jiang Qi woke to Ke Yan's kiss and groggily pushed at his shoulder.

Ke Yan lifted the hem of his pajama top and slid a hand beneath it, stroking softly; when his fingers reached the side of Jiang Qi's waist and pinched lightly, a melting weakness spread through him, leaving him pliant and powerless.

Ke Yan left his lips and kissed lower, following the jaw down to the throat, licking and biting, printing red mark after red mark — vivid against the older ones already faded to bruise-purple. At his chest, he opened his mouth and bit.

"Mn— Ke Yan." Jiang Qi came awake all at once, his face slowly reddening.

Ke Yan kissed his lips again. "Good boy. Say 'Yan.'"

"Mm... Yan."

"Mm..." A sweet, clinging sound escaped Jiang Qi, both hands gripping the bare skin of Ke Yan's back.

"Ah, wait — wait." Jiang Qi stopped him. "A condom..."

"We're out," Ke Yan answered swiftly. And pressed forward...

Afterward, Jiang Qi lay limp in his arms. "So tired. My back's killing me."

Ke Yan reached over to knead it. "You didn't even have to move, and you're still tired?"

Jiang Qi rolled over to sprawl on top of him. "Then next time I'm on top."

"Don't even dream about it, wife." Ke Yan patted his waist, teasing.

Sulking, Jiang Qi pinched him. "I want a bath."

"All right." Ke Yan lifted him off the bed and carried him toward the bathroom.

Ding-dong— They'd just finished bathing and were dressing when the downstairs bell rang. Ke Yan pulled on his shirt. "I'll get it."

"Young Master—" At the door stood the cook from the old Ke residence. Everyone called her Auntie Li; she had a sunny disposition, smiling all day, and her husband was the Ke family's steward, so Ke Yan knew her well.

"Come in."

The two walked into the living room just as Jiang Qi came down the stairs. Seeing a stranger, he paused.

Auntie Li was the more startled of the two. How is there someone else in the Young Master's home? A man, no less, and in housewear? A friend? Several questions flashed through her mind, though she asked none of them.

"And this is?" Jiang Qi asked.

"Auntie Li — she cooks." Ke Yan finished and went to sit on the sofa.

"Hello, Mr. Jiang — just call me Auntie Li."

"Then call me Jiang Qi."

"All right — Jiang Qi," she said with a warm little laugh. The more she looked at this boy, the more she liked him — clean-featured, easy to get along with. What a pity he isn't a girl; the Young Master could have taken him for a wife. Then her eye caught the blotches on his neck, red and quite a few of them, some blue-purple too. "Jiang Qi, dear — is your neck reacting to something? Insect bites? Shall I fetch something to put on it?"

Jiang Qi was at a loss, and covered his neck a touch awkwardly.

Ke Yan came to the rescue. "It's nothing — insect bites. He's already put medicine on it."

"Oh, good. I'll go start the cooking, then."

Jiang Qi sat down beside Ke Yan. "By the way — what's brought on hiring Auntie Li all of a sudden?"

"I was afraid you'd burn the kitchen down one of these days." Ke Yan paused. "And that you'd cut your hand open again." He couldn't help laughing — and it was hard to begrudge him; a man who could slice his finger open merely washing his hands was a rare specimen indeed.

Jiang Qi went red and reached over to pinch his arm. "I'm going to go help Auntie Li cook." And he fled toward the kitchen.

Ke Yan didn't stop him — he knew Jiang Qi was certain to be chased right back out.

In the kitchen, Auntie Li was cutting vegetables when Ke Yan's laughter from the other room brought her up short, a faintly incredulous look crossing her face. The cold, cold Young Master? she wondered, baffled. Just then Jiang Qi came in. "Auntie Li, let me help."

"All right," she said with a sunny smile.

And soon enough — sure enough — Jiang Qi was driven out of the kitchen by an Auntie Li wielding a spatula, muttering as she went: absolutely cannot let him in here again.

He shuffled dejectedly back to Ke Yan, who reached out and ruffled his hair. "There now. Just stay by my side and watch some TV."

Auntie Li returned to the old Ke residence — a house in a blended Sino-European style — and went into one of its rooms. Inside, a beautiful, elegant young woman reclined against a soft sofa, reading a comic book. Auntie Li reported to her, point by point, everything she'd seen that day.

The woman gave an interested little "Oh?" This Jiang Qi — I'd quite like to see what he looks like.
