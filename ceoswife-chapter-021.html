The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 21

Ye Xingwei 叶星伟

July 2026

~6 min read

The helicopter descended slowly. The people on the training ground had long since drawn off to one side, buzzing over what was going on. The students were thrilled — the girls over what sort of person might emerge, the boys over the helicopter itself; Jiang Qi was thrilled too, because it meant a chance to rest.

Watching the eager faces around him, Jiang Qi was suddenly seized by the wish to know what expression Ke Yan wore just now. Curiosity? Excitement? Or the same flat, couldn't-care-less look as always? But he was disappointed — all he saw was Ke Yan's back, walking off the ground.

"Whoa." A wave of astonished gasps pulled his gaze back. The helicopter door had opened. First down came two men in black suits, and after them... a cane??? Or rather, a person leaning on one — legs bound in bandages, face hidden behind a large pair of sunglasses, back stooped over the cane. The two men in black helped him to a chair at the side and sat him down.

The students blinked. What was this? The airs, the production — like a wounded crime boss come to school for a holiday.

"Young Master, your phone." One of the men in black handed it over. The other set down two oversized suitcases, and the pair traded a glance and walked off without a word.

"Stop right there." Ye Xingwei, in the chair, lost his composure. He hadn't wanted to come to this dump of a school; the old man had forced him, and when he wouldn't come, had had people abduct him here, confiscating his phone, laptop, and bank cards. And now they meant to leave him to fend for himself? "Where's my card?"

"In the bag. Also — forgot to mention: the master said you'll still get your money, to spend however you like, but apart from money you'll be given no help whatsoever. You're to shape up and finish your studies, or face the consequences yourself." And the man left without waiting for a reply.

The students who'd overheard wore looks of dawning comprehension — a rich young master, forced here to study.

Ye Xingwei sat fuming, fists clenched till the veins stood out. Forgot to tell me? They knew all along I'd lose my temper.

"Why's he come to our school?" Jiang Qi asked Meng Qing — Meng Qing being so well-connected he knew nearly every piece of gossip going, girls' or boys'; ask him, and most of the time he'd have the answer.

"I don't know either." Meng Qing waved a hand.

"Don't you call yourself the know-it-all?"

"Even a know-it-all gets clogged up like a toilet sometimes."

Jiang Qi and Liu Hao wrinkled their noses — what a vulgar comparison.

Irritated by the murmuring crowd, Ye Xingwei slammed the cane against the ground and reached for his bandaged leg. To everyone's alarm he unwound the bandages — revealing a leg perfectly intact, not injured in the slightest. What's the point of faking an injury? Jiang Qi wondered.

Bandages off, Ye Xingwei was no longer so irritable. He stood, removed the sunglasses, and noted with great satisfaction the dazzled looks his face drew. He gave the crowd a mocking little smile, picked up the suitcases, and strode off with a flourish.

"What arrogance," Jiang Qi let the words drift out slowly.

Meng Qing clapped his shoulder. "Well, he's got the capital for it."

"Right? Honestly, it's infuriating," Liu Hao chimed in.

As for Ye Xingwei's arrival — the girls were wholeheartedly in favor, the boys aggrieved. One Ke Yan already, and now another; the girls' very souls were being charmed away. How was a fellow supposed to find a girlfriend? Liu Hao was a card-carrying member of the aggrieved legion.

That evening, training over, the three came back and showered, then sat chatting. Liu Hao lay on his bed, the picture of discontent. "Two rich, handsome heartthrobs turn up out of nowhere — what's a nobody like me to do?"

"What do you mean, do? You live your life, they live theirs — no connection." Drying his hair, Jiang Qi couldn't see what it had to do with Liu Hao.

"How is there no connection? Because of them the girls look down on me even more. I was all set to find a pretty girlfriend, and now the pretty ones won't give me a second glance."

"Then have Meng Qing introduce you to someone — doesn't he know loads of girls?"

Liu Hao's eyes lit up. Meng Qing shrugged. "The girls I know all asked me to investigate Ke Yan — and now it's investigate Ke Yan and today's arrogant little punk. Best give it up."

Liu Hao went straight back to being aggrieved.

Bang bang— someone kicking the dorm door.

"Who is it, the rude—" Liu Hao shouted, in a foul mood.

"No idea, I'll look." Jiang Qi opened the door — to find Ye Xingwei. "Can I help you?"

"I live here."

"Huh?" Jiang Qi clearly hadn't expected that.

"Move. I'm coming in."

"Oh."

At Ye Xingwei's moving in, the three felt thoroughly out of sorts — and worn out from the day besides, so they turned in early.

In the dead of night Jiang Qi opened his eyes in a temper and glared at the upper bunk, the source of an unceasing noise. Unable to bear it, he flipped over, sat up, grabbed the flashlight at his bedside, and hurled it with all his might at the person clattering away on a laptop above. "Can't you fucking type tomorrow? Are you going to let people sleep or not?"

Ye Xingwei jumped half out of his skin. In his impression Jiang Qi was a thin, slight, delicate-featured boy; he'd never imagined being hit — with who-knew-what, painful enough besides — and cursed at on top of it.

Seeing him go quiet, Jiang Qi retrieved the flashlight, lay back, and went on sleeping.

Across the room, Liu Hao and Meng Qing had been woken by the outburst, and looked at Ye Xingwei with sympathy; they too had felt the wrath of Jiang Qi's wake-up temper — Liu Hao hit with clothes, Meng Qing with a pillow, neither hurting. Ye Xingwei had it worst, struck with a flashlight. That surely smarted.

Jiang Qi's wake-up temper, it seemed, was a fearsome thing. Whatever you do, don't provoke him in his sleep, and don't wake him — or the consequences will be dire.

The next morning, the moment Ye Xingwei opened his eyes he saw Jiang Qi's face. Jiang Qi stood by the bed and stammered, "Um — about last night, I'm really sorry. I didn't hurt you, did I?" Liu Hao and Meng Qing had told him only that he'd thrown something in the night, not what.

Ye Xingwei touched the shoulder that had taken the hit; it still ached a little. But seeing Jiang Qi's face flushed red with remorse — and being, for all his temper, not actually bad-hearted — he said, "Forget it. It didn't hurt much."

Jiang Qi breathed a sigh of relief and gave him an embarrassed smile. And at that smile, the discontent that had built in Ye Xingwei over these past days seemed to be smiled clean away.
