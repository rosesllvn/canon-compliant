The CEO’s Wife Is a Man

Chapter 150

Auntie 婶婶

July 2026

~6 min read

Lin Zirui and Yang Shaoyu rose early that weekend to visit the Yang house. Yang's eldest brother was home and wanted to meet Lin Zirui.

Yang's eldest brother rose early every day at home, because Father Yang hated anyone sleeping in — get up late and you'd miss not just breakfast but, if the old man was in a mood, lunch too.

So however much he liked to sleep in, back home he was up early.

Today, however, Father Yang had gone out at dawn to admire a neighbor's new fishing rod, so the early rising was for nothing.

Yang Shaoyu and Lin Zirui also rose early, reaching the Yang house at just past nine.

Lin Zirui followed Yang Shaoyu in, and saw at a glance the eldest brother on the sofa.

The eldest brother studied the man Yang Shaoyu had brought: fair-skinned, delicate-featured, a doctor from what he'd heard, and a little thinner than Shaoyu.

"Zirui, this is my eldest brother. Eldest Brother, this is the person I love, Lin Zirui." Yang Shaoyu introduced him with great solemnity.

The eldest brother hadn't expected such earnestness; he raised a brow and looked Lin Zirui over. Someone who could make his second brother this serious was no ordinary person.

Lin Zirui hadn't expected the introduction either, and flushed, but did as Yang Shaoyu said. "Hello, Eldest Brother — I'm Lin Zirui."

"I'm Shaoyu's eldest brother. I've never seen Shaoyu so serious about anyone. You two take good care of each other from now on." The eldest brother adapted fast, already putting on big-brother airs.

"Don't worry, Eldest Brother — we will." Lin Zirui and Yang Shaoyu assured him.

"Good." He nodded.

"Are Shaoyu and Zirui here?" Mother Yang and the eldest sister-in-law came out of the kitchen at the sound.

"This is your sister-in-law." Yang Shaoyu meant the young woman beside Mother Yang.

"Mom, Sister-in-law — I've brought Zirui." Yang Shaoyu greeted first, lest Lin Zirui feel awkward.

"Mom, Sister-in-law." Lin Zirui followed.

Mother Yang had met Lin Zirui before and talked with him, so she smiled and nodded.

The sister-in-law didn't bristle at being called that, smiling back. "I hear you're a doctor — you must be busy."

"Yes, though I get a day or two off each week."

"Your father caught a lot of fish yesterday — I'll make you fish soup today, to build you up; Shaoyu says your health isn't great." Mother Yang was a fine cook; she'd just grown lazy about it these years, with a cook and servants in the house — but today, with Shaoyu bringing Zirui home, she wanted to cook a meal for him to taste.

"Thank you, Mom." Lin Zirui had never known a mother's home cooking. The promise of it filled his chest with a bittersweet warmth that brought tears close.

"Zirui, you're in for a treat today — Mom's fish is the best." The eldest brother hadn't had his mother's cooking in a long while either.

"Yes — I'm truly fortunate." No sooner said than his hand was taken by Yang Shaoyu, whose warm palm passed its heat into Lin Zirui's, gripping tight, as if to draw out all of Lin Zirui's cold and leave him only warmth.

The eldest brother saw the small gesture and realized that the younger brother who had always sworn he'd never marry had truly grown up. Shaoyu had become considerate and gentle, had learned to cherish the person he loved, and had brought him home. Lin Zirui was a man—but what did that matter when his brother loved him this much?

Mother Yang went back to cooking, the sister-in-law of course following to help, the cook lending a hand at the side.

Father Yang came back then, and seeing Lin Zirui and Yang Shaoyu, laughed heartily. "I thought you wouldn't be back till the afternoon — and Zirui works so hard, on a rare day off he should sleep in, no need to rush over."

"It's fine, Dad — I rested well, so I wanted to come early." Lin Zirui liked Father Yang's easygoing, blunt manner.

"Good."

The eldest brother, meanwhile, wondered whether this was really his father — "should sleep in," indeed; he'd never once said such a thing to him; whenever he got up late his father glared daggers at him. Such favoritism.

"Oh — where's Xiao De?" Father Yang sat down and didn't see his little grandson.

"Still doing homework in his room — should be nearly done." The eldest brother wasn't strict about the child's studies; the boy was small, and as long as he finished what the teacher set, the eldest brother didn't push him into extra lessons.

As they spoke, the eldest brother's son, Yang Wende, came pattering down the stairs. Everyone looked over; Yang Wende, seeing the living room so full, paused — then spotted Yang Shaoyu, and on his little short legs ran straight to him, arms up to be held.

Yang Shaoyu scooped him up, and Xiao De wrapped his little arms around his neck and called sweetly, "Uncle."

"Xiao De's such a good boy." Xiao De was Yang Wende's nickname.

Then Xiao De noticed Lin Zirui and fixed his curious gaze on the stranger beside his uncle. He didn't think he had seen this big brother before.

Lin Zirui smiled at him, not quite sure how to greet a child so small.

Father Yang felt a touch put out. "Xiao De, why hug your uncle and not your grandpa?"

"Xiao De hasn't seen Uncle in so long." Xiao De said it, eyes still on Lin Zirui.

Then Yang Shaoyu plopped Xiao De into Lin Zirui's arms. Lin Zirui blinked, then held him.

Xiao De wasn't shy at all, blinking his big dark eyes up at Lin Zirui from his arms, grabbing a fistful of Lin Zirui's sweater, looking from his uncle to Lin Zirui — this big brother seemed to be Uncle's friend — and asked, "Uncle, is this big brother your friend?"

Yang Shaoyu glanced at Lin Zirui and smiled. "No — he's the one your uncle loves. He's your uncle's wife."

"Wife? Does that mean like a wifey?" Xiao De often heard his dad call his mom "wife" or "wifey," so little as he was, he knew what it meant.

"That's right — clever Xiao De." Yang Shaoyu pinched his cheek.

Father Yang and the eldest brother laughed too — what a sharp little thing.

"Then should I call this big brother Auntie?" Xiao De's next words froze everyone for a beat, and made Lin Zirui flush.

Yang Shaoyu liked the title, and dipped to kiss Xiao De's cheek. "Clever boy — how did you know to call him Auntie?"

Praised, Xiao De lifted his face proudly. "I read it in a book — Uncle's wife is called Auntie." Xiao De hadn't minded that the long-haired lady-auntie in the book had become a man-auntie.

Lin Zirui, past his embarrassment, smiled too, and gave Xiao De's little hand a gentle squeeze. Such a clever child.

Father Yang and the eldest brother had no fear that knowing the truth would somehow make Xiao De turn out like his uncle, nor would they hide Lin Zirui's place in the family. He was the person Yang Shaoyu meant to spend his life with; treating him with kindness and respect was the least their family could do.
